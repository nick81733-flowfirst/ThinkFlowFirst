Doctor-Partnered Outlets vs Owned Centers

A staged market-entry framework: prioritize existing doctor-operated clinics at launch; retain independently owned FlowFirst Centers as a later option once demand, regulatory feasibility and operating economics are proven.

Recommended direction — for stakeholder discussion, not a final commitment.

Begin with a suitably licensed and qualified doctor-partnered clinic. Keep product supply, clinical services, marketing and regional business development commercially distinct. Do not commit to an owned center or grant an introducer distribution rights merely for making a connection. Reassess ownership after sustained volume and audited financial results.

1. What we are trying to accomplish

Launch a capital-light JB access point with professionally delivered, legally authorized infusions; test demand; protect FFG and Malaysia ECMP margins; compensate doctors fairly for actual facilities and clinical responsibilities; and build a replicable clinic network. Owning our own outlet remains a benchmark and possible next phase, not the initial default.

4. Partner doctor: scope and compensation

The doctor-partner clinic remains responsible for clinical eligibility decisions, prescribing where lawful, trained staff, monitoring, records, adverse-event response and facilities. For planning only, the package operator pays RM100 per completed infusion (RM2,000/customer) for documented clinic/facility/clinical support obligations. Separately, the treating clinic retains the whole RM100 patient clinical administration fee on each completed infusion and covers its own nursing cost. Thus clinic gross receipts are RM200 per completed infusion, before its own clinical operating expenses. The two fee streams require clear non-overlapping descriptions and a legally reviewed agreement; neither is a referral reward.

8. Why partner-first remains attractive

Lower initial capital, less direct responsibility for fixed doctor/nurse payroll, access to an existing doctor and clinic, and a chance to test patient demand and clinical operating procedures before constructing our own center. We should not interpret the higher modeled owned-center contribution at ten or more customers as an immediate instruction to build: the assumed RM35,000 fixed monthly cost may omit capital recovery, relief staffing, compliance infrastructure, and extra staff/chairs.

When should an owned center be reconsidered?

After consistent verified demand, positive unit economics, a legally feasible licensed operating model, demonstrated clinical protocols and product authorization, reliable staffing arrangements, and a fully costed startup/working-capital plan. A realistic comparison should use 12-month cash flow and an explicit capital payback period, not only monthly contributions.

9. Launch roadmap and stakeholder decisions

  1. Regulatory and clinical gate: obtain Malaysian legal/regulatory advice on product classification, lawful supply/administration, clinic permissions, advertising and clinical protocols. Do not proceed to infusions until authorized and safety issues are resolved.
  2. Clinic partner negotiation: define real responsibilities behind RM100 clinic service fee, confirm RM100 patient visit fee retained by treating clinic, define supplies, monitoring, staffing, reporting, liability and patient consent.
  3. ECMP/introducer agreement: preserve 5% economic equivalent funded from ECMP's existing US$25 spread; pay only for lawful, verifiable services and prohibit remuneration for patient referrals or prescribing. No ECMP title or equity initially.
  4. Marketing controls: review 25% budget, separate media spend from agency fee, track cost per qualified enquiry and customer; ensure healthcare advertising compliance.
  5. Pilot budget: cost chairs, medical equipment, emergency readiness, product wastage, freight, storage, insurance, support and extra clinic/central overhead.
  6. Review after real operation: assess sales demand, completion rates, clinical quality and true contribution before any own-center commitment.
Discussion recommendation

Adopt the doctor-partnered outlet as the proposed Phase 1 entry route, keeping S$4,800 plus RM100/visit as a test price. Retain the fully owned FlowFirst Center as Phase 2 comparator. Seek stakeholder agreement on commercial responsibilities, regulatory feasibility, patient pricing and a properly costed pilot before approving launch.
