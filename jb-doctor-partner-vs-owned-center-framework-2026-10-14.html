Doctor-Partnered Outlets vs Owned Centers

A staged market-entry framework: prioritize existing doctor-operated clinics at launch; retain independently owned FlowFirst Centers as a later option once demand, regulatory feasibility and operating economics are proven.

Recommended direction — for stakeholder discussion, not a final commitment.

Begin with a suitably licensed and qualified doctor-partnered clinic. Keep product supply, clinical services, marketing and regional business development commercially distinct. Do not commit to an owned center or grant an introducer distribution rights merely for making a connection. Reassess ownership after sustained volume and audited financial results.

1. What we are trying to accomplish

Launch a capital-light JB access point with professionally delivered, legally authorized infusions; test demand; protect FFG and Malaysia ECMP margins; compensate doctors fairly for actual facilities and clinical responsibilities; and build a replicable clinic network. Owning our own outlet remains a benchmark and possible next phase, not the initial default.

2. Agreed all-inclusive packages and marketing structure

One customer-facing package price includes clinical administration and standard supplies. There is no separate RM100 clinic administration charge to add at checkout. All commissions are calculated only on the therapy component, after excluding S$30 per infusion.

3. Upstream supply and distribution

FFG's assumed product cost is US$20 per infusion, its transfer to Malaysia ECMP is US$50, and ECMP's supply price to either type of JB outlet is US$75 per infusion. FFG's gross spread is US$30; ECMP's gross spread is US$25 before its agreed introducer payment.

The older separate ECMP-funded introducer arrangement survives: 5% of the S$4,800 therapy amount (S$240 per 20-pack), funded solely by ECMP. It is not deducted again from the JB operator or owned-center P&L, and it is distinct from the Patricia/Michael combined override inside the 30% downstream marketing allocation.

4. Doctor-partner clinic — agreed scope and fee

The independent clinic receives a single RM200 per completed infusion, entirely paid from the S$5,400 all-inclusive package proceeds. This covers clinical assessment and supervision as applicable, nurse administration, standard supplies, facilities, monitoring and delivery responsibilities. It is not RM100 + RM100 collected from the patient; that division is retired. The doctor remains responsible for individual clinical eligibility, prescribing decisions where required and clinical safety. The commercial rate is agreed for negotiation and must be specified in the signed clinic agreement.

Assumes 26 operating days per month, 8 hours per day, and 90-minute chair occupation per infusion (additional turnaround time would increase needs). A three-chair clinic at ten new monthly customers averages about 7.7 infusions/day. Nursing coverage must be based on actual clinical workflow, concurrent patient monitoring, leave and adverse-event preparedness—not merely insertion/removal minutes. Staffing and space cost in the owned model may rise for 15–20 customers.

8. Why partner-first remains attractive

Lower initial capital, less direct responsibility for fixed doctor/nurse payroll, access to an existing doctor and clinic, and a chance to test patient demand and clinical operating procedures before constructing our own center. We should not interpret the higher modeled owned-center contribution at ten or more customers as an immediate instruction to build: the assumed RM35,000 fixed monthly cost may omit capital recovery, relief staffing, compliance infrastructure, and extra staff/chairs.

When should an owned center be reconsidered?

After consistent verified demand, positive unit economics, a legally feasible licensed operating model, demonstrated clinical protocols and product authorization, reliable staffing arrangements, and a fully costed startup/working-capital plan. A realistic comparison should use 12-month cash flow and an explicit capital payback period, not only monthly contributions.

9. Launch roadmap and stakeholder decisions

  1. Confirm the lawful product and clinical route, with appropriate training, procedures and informed consent.
  2. Contract RM200 per infusion inclusive of partner clinic medical delivery, nurse administration, standard supplies and facilities; no extra standard fee collected from customer.
  3. Specify 15% first-line, 5% second-line, 5% Patricia and Michael combined override and 5% general marketing budget, all solely on therapy-only price.
  4. Continue the historical separate 5% introducer fee, paid out of ECMP's own margin, keeping it outside JB operator accounts.
  5. Fully cost any owned-center alternative with variable supplies, suitable staffing and equipment/capital before declaring it superior.
Recommended approach

Use existing doctor-partnered outlets initially at S$1,550 / S$2,900 / S$5,400 all-inclusive. The 20-infusion package is the standard 5–20-customer modeling unit. An owned FlowFirst clinic remains an alternative only once actual comparable full costs and capacity are validated.
