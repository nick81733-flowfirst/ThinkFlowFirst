What Is Microcirculation?

What happens after blood leaves the big vessels?

Microcirculation includes the small vessels closest to tissues—arterioles, capillaries and venules. Large vessels bring blood toward an organ; microcirculation helps determine what happens closer to the tissue itself.

A normal pulse-oximeter reading can be useful, but it does not by itself prove that every tissue is receiving and using oxygen perfectly. Tissue oxygenation also depends on blood flow, distribution through small vessels, diffusion and cellular use.

Key takeaway: Large vessels bring blood toward an organ. Microcirculation helps determine what happens closer to the tissue itself.

Look a little closer

Imagine looking at a country from space. You can see the major highways, but not whether every home receives supplies. Large arteries are essential, yet they tell only part of the delivery story.

Capillaries are tiny vessels where much of the exchange between blood and tissue takes place. Their function depends on more than their number: distribution, pressure, permeability and the surrounding tissue all matter.
