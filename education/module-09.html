Flow and Recovery

Why do some people recover better than others after illness or injury?

Recovery can involve controlling inflammation, clearing damaged material, rebuilding, adaptation and relearning. Tissues need oxygen and nutrients during these processes, so circulation is one part of the recovery environment—but it is not the only factor.

Stroke illustrates the point. Recovery may involve brain injury, rehabilitation and neuroadaptation over months or years. The importance of blood supply does not itself prove that a particular flow-focused therapy improves stroke recovery.

Key takeaway: Recovery needs more than time. It needs the right support—and circulation is one important part of that support.

Look a little closer

Recovery is not a single event. It can involve healing damaged tissue, restoring strength, retraining movement and learning to adapt to changes.

Good recovery support therefore asks more than one question: is the underlying condition being treated, is rehabilitation appropriate, is nutrition adequate, is sleep sufficient and are complications being monitored?
