FlowFirst Channel × Demand Entry Door Framework

A practical planning framework for deciding which kind of message belongs on which channel. Instead of treating Facebook, TikTok, doctors, seminars or WhatsApp as separate strategies, FlowFirst uses each channel to open one or more Demand Entry Doors into a common education, qualification and follow-up system.

Core principle: Many channels. Many Demand Entry Doors. One FlowFirst education and qualification system.

1. What is a Demand Entry Door?

A Demand Entry Door is the reason a person chooses to engage with FlowFirst. The channel is simply where they encounter us. A person may enter because they are curious, because they have a problem, because they are facing a decision, because they need navigation, because they want evidence, or because someone they trust referred them.

The channel tells us where we meet the person. The Demand Entry Door tells us why they engage.

This distinction matters because the same channel can support several different entry motivations. Facebook is not only an “awareness” channel. A doctor is not merely a “referral source.” WhatsApp is not only a follow-up tool. Each can support different doors depending on the audience and the message.

2. The six core Demand Entry Doors

Door 1

Curiosity

“Tell me more.” Questions, overlooked perspectives and pattern interrupts that earn attention without needing to explain everything publicly.

Door 2

Problem

“I have this issue.” Start with the person’s real difficulty, such as stalled recovery, recurring symptoms, poor function or uncertainty.

Door 3

Decision

“I am about to choose.” Reach people before surgery, another specialist, overseas treatment, a major spend or giving up.

Door 4

Navigation

“I do not know what to do next.” Help patients and families understand options, sequence, referrals and next steps.

Door 5

Evidence

“Show me why I should believe this.” Use science, mechanisms, publications, expert explanation and clearly labelled real-world experience.

Door 6

Referral / Community

“Someone I trust introduced me.” Use doctors, physiotherapists, partners, support groups, family networks and community relationships.

4. How the digital marketing team should use the matrix

The operating question is not simply “What should we post on Facebook?” It is:

For this channel, which Demand Entry Door are we opening, for whom, and what is the next step after they respond?

Examples:

  • TikTok / Shorts: lead mainly with Curiosity, supported by simple Problem or Decision messages.
  • Facebook / Instagram: test Curiosity, Problem and Decision as separate creative families instead of mixing them into one message.
  • YouTube: use longer Problem, Decision and Evidence content that can explain context more fully.
  • Google / Search: meet people who already have intent. Problem, Decision, Navigation and Evidence are stronger than broad curiosity.
  • WhatsApp / Telegram: use as a high-trust conversation channel for Problem, Decision, Navigation and referral-driven enquiries.
  • ThinkFlowFirst.com: support the deeper educational journey across Problem, Decision, Navigation and Evidence.

5. Offline channels belong in the same system

FlowFirst should not think of demand creation as digital-only. In healthcare, trust often travels through people and institutions before it travels through advertising. Doctors, physiotherapists, rehabilitation providers, home-care companies, seminars, support groups, churches, senior groups and printed QR materials can all be acquisition channels.

A doctor may open the Problem, Decision, Navigation, Evidence or Referral door. A seminar may open the Evidence or Navigation door. A waiting-room card may open the Decision door. These should all feed into the same FlowFirst follow-up and attribution system.

6. This is not the conventional marketing funnel

Traditional digital marketing often maps channels against stages such as Awareness, Consideration and Conversion. That remains useful, but it does not fully explain why a person first chooses to engage.

FlowFirst therefore adds another planning layer: entry motivation. The six Demand Entry Doors describe the psychological reason for engagement. The conventional funnel can still describe what happens afterward.

FlowFirst adaptation: Channel → Demand Entry Door → Conversation / Education → Qualification → Appropriate Next Step → Follow-up.

8. What should be measured

Each test should be tagged by Channel + Demand Entry Door + Audience + Creative + Outcome. This allows FlowFirst to learn not only which platform produces leads, but which entry motivation produces the best qualified conversations and customers.

  • Cost per response / enquiry
  • Cost per qualified lead
  • Response-to-conversation rate
  • Qualified-conversation-to-consultation rate
  • Consultation-to-customer rate
  • Cost per acquired customer
  • Time to conversion
  • Door × channel combinations that produce the highest-quality demand

9. Relationship to the Curiosity-Led Demand Funnel

The earlier Curiosity-Led Demand Funnel Framework remains important, but curiosity is now understood as one Demand Entry Door among several. It is particularly useful for public social platforms, but it should not become the only acquisition strategy.

FlowFirst should be able to meet an early adopter through curiosity, a cautious family through evidence, a confused caregiver through navigation, a patient at a decision point through “Before you… Think Flow First,” and a referred patient through a trusted clinician — then bring all of them into the same education and qualification system.

10. Operating principle

Do not use one message everywhere. Match the Demand Entry Door to the channel, audience and moment. Then measure which combinations create meaningful conversations and real outcomes.
