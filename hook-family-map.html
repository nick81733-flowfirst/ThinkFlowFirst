One brand idea. Many entry points. Think Flow First.

What lives here?

This library combines the hook family map and the master hook repository. A hook family is a repeatable way into the conversation; the individual lines beneath it are the approved or developing executions.

The exact words can change by condition, market and channel while the underlying structure stays recognisably Think Flow First.

The operating rule

Do not start by asking, “What ad should we write?”

Start with: Who are we speaking to, what are they thinking right now, and which hook family matches that moment?

Two big audience zones

Almost every hook sits primarily on one side of this divide. The creative family then determines the angle.

Zone A

Stay Well

People thinking about prevention, risk, longevity, healthspan, ageing, aesthetics, biohacking and staying independent before a major event happens.

Zone B

Get More Help

People already dealing with stroke, recovery, chronic problems, stalled progress, exhausted options or a major treatment decision.

Public-facing health copy: one important guardrail

Strong hooks should create attention, curiosity and a reason to ask questions. They should not turn emerging evidence, individual outcomes or a novel therapy into a guaranteed diagnosis, cure or treatment result. In public-facing copy, prefer language such as “approach,” “explore,” “support,” “learn,” “ask,” and “could” where the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.

How the team should use this library

1. Identify the audience moment: staying well or seeking more help. 2. Pick one hook family that matches that moment. 3. Add one tactical angle. 4. Adapt the condition, goal, therapy or decision. 5. Prefer locked lines where available. 6. End with the simplest appropriate Think Flow First call to action.

The objective is not to make every ad sound identical. It is to make every ad feel like it came from the same idea and the same brand.
