Master Hook Library

The single source of truth for Think Flow First hooks. The former Hook Family Map is now incorporated here: families, locked lines, formulas, examples and messaging guardrails live together in one working master.

One brand idea. Many entry points. Think Flow First.

What lives here?

This library combines the hook family map and the master hook repository. A hook family is a repeatable way into the conversation; the individual lines beneath it are the approved or developing executions.

The exact words can change by condition, market and channel while the underlying structure stays recognisably Think Flow First.

The operating rule

Do not start by asking, “What ad should we write?”

Start with: Who are we speaking to, what are they thinking right now, and which hook family matches that moment?

Two big audience zones

Almost every hook sits primarily on one side of this divide. The creative family then determines the angle.

Zone A

Stay Well

People thinking about prevention, risk, longevity, healthspan, ageing, aesthetics, biohacking and staying independent before a major event happens.

Zone B

Get More Help

People already dealing with stroke, recovery, chronic problems, stalled progress, exhausted options or a major treatment decision.

October campaign execution notes

Working instructions for the digital marketing team; not additional claims about the therapy.

Campaign priorities

Stroke: test stronger technology-led curiosity

Keep the previously discussed campaign themes where still useful, but replace the mild generic Campaign B with the technology-led family (22). Compare it with the decision-point, stalled-progress and sharing families. Label exact unapproved copy as a test, not a locked hook.

Funnel & operations

Public curiosity → private human response

Public posts can invite viewers to comment FLOW or send a direct message. Initially manage replies manually, capture source and consent, and share accurate educational information on follow-up. If volume justifies it, revisit ManyChat. Do not make misleading claims in private responses that would be inappropriate publicly.

Creative formats

Test static, text and short video

Use strong readable static imagery and short caption variants alongside realistic, subtitled short-form video. Measure meaningful conversations and qualified enquiries, not views alone. Avoid exaggerated before/after material or testimonial claims.

Production & approvals

Manual deployment only

GitHub edits are source changes. Render publication must be triggered manually, then the live page checked. Do not assume that a GitHub commit is automatically published. Preserve official brand logo, typography, colours, and tagline in campaign production.

Public-facing health copy: one important guardrail

Strong hooks should create attention, curiosity and a reason to ask questions. Never advise delaying standard care, especially urgent stroke evaluation. They should not turn emerging evidence, individual outcomes or a novel therapy into a guaranteed diagnosis, cure or treatment result. In public-facing copy, prefer language such as “approach,” “explore,” “support,” “learn,” “ask,” and “could” where the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.

How the team should use this library

1. Identify the audience moment: staying well or seeking more help. 2. Pick one hook family that matches that moment. 3. Add one tactical angle. 4. Adapt the condition, goal, therapy or decision. 5. Prefer locked lines where available. 6. End with the simplest appropriate Think Flow First call to action.

The objective is not to make every ad sound identical. It is to make every ad feel like it came from the same idea and the same brand.
