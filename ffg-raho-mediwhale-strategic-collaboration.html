Exploring a partnership in AI-powered vascular risk assessment, credible clinical research, physician engagement and international commercialization.

Prepared for stakeholder discussion | Exploratory proposal — no partnership or clinical effectiveness claim is implied

Executive Summary

Mediwhale's non-invasive retinal AI risk-assessment platform could complement two distinct missions: RAHO's pursuit of rigorous evidence regarding investigational IGDS nanobubble technology, and Flow First Global's work building responsible international clinical relationships and commercial channels.

The opportunity should be structured as two independent workstreams: (1) commercial access to established retinal screening technology and (2) carefully designed, independently governed research. Neither Mediwhale's screening results nor its regulatory clearances demonstrate that IGDS is effective or authorized.

1. Why Mediwhale is relevant

Its profile identifies three products: Dr. Noon CVD, which predicts cardiovascular risk from retinal photographs; Dr. Noon Fundus, supporting eye-abnormality screening; and Dr. Noon CKD, which the company describes as under development and not commercially available. The company also lists proposed CVD Plus, Liver and Brain developments.

Retinal images provide a non-invasive view of microvascular structures. Mediwhale reports published work comparing the risk-prediction performance of Dr. Noon CVD and approaches involving coronary artery calcium. Importantly, risk prediction is different from diagnosis of a particular lesion or measurement of the response to an infusion.

Source: Mediwhale company profile supplied for review, particularly pp. 9–16, 20. Claims about regulatory status, product performance or availability need verification for the specific product version and territory before use in public materials.

2. Six collaboration opportunities

A. RAHO evidence program

Explore whether retinal AI measures can be incorporated into prospective IGDS studies with clinical comparators, independent oversight and publication standards.

B. FlowFirst screening network

Offer clinician-supervised cardiovascular risk screening through appropriately licensed medical and screening partners, separate from IGDS eligibility or sales.

C. International distribution

Explore available country rights, software licensing, camera integration and FFG-assisted introductions to new hospitals and screening clinics.

D. Physician engagement

Use an established screening use case to begin evidence-led discussions with cardiovascular, metabolic, ophthalmology and preventive-health clinicians.

E. Cerebrovascular research

Consider future stroke-risk research, including potential future brain-related products, while avoiding unsupported claims about stroke rehabilitation monitoring.

F. Biomarker alliance

With independent researchers, investigate retinal microvascular features, conventional physiological measures and possible longitudinal outcomes; agree data and intellectual-property rights first.

3. Priority: research on change over time

The most promising scientific question is not whether a low or high Dr. Noon CVD score justifies IGDS — it does not. It is whether retinal imaging offers a reproducible, relevant research measure alongside recognized cardiovascular endpoints in properly designed studies.

QuestionResearch objectiveImportant caveat
FeasibilityCan baseline and serial retinal images be obtained consistently?Image quality and repeatability must be measured.
Biological relevanceDo retinal measures correlate with independently measured cardiovascular variables?Correlation cannot establish causation.
Clinical utilityDo biomarker changes predict validated patient outcomes in controlled studies?A score shift does not prove vascular clearance, disease reversal or therapeutic benefit.

Illustrative study sequence

  1. Independent investigators specify hypotheses, eligibility, primary endpoints, safety monitoring and comparator strategy.
  2. Collect baseline retinal imaging and standard cardiovascular measures.
  3. Conduct any investigational intervention only after required ethics and regulatory review and informed consent.
  4. Repeat assessments at justified intervals, potentially 1, 3 and 6 months.
  5. Analyze adverse events and all prespecified outcomes independently, including negative or inconclusive findings.
Scientific safeguard: Dr. Noon CVD is a cardiovascular risk-prediction tool. It must not be assumed responsive to short-term therapy or valid as a surrogate endpoint for IGDS without separate validation.

4. FFG commercial pathway

A possible FlowFirst Vascular Health Network could connect participating clinical screening facilities to physician-led preventive care. The stages should be:

  1. Non-invasive retinal examination at an appropriately licensed partner site.
  2. Physician review of cardiovascular risk and other clinically relevant data.
  3. Evidence-based preventive management and appropriate referrals.
  4. Optional, separately consented introduction to independently approved research studies where applicable — never automatic conversion to an investigational infusion.

Potential FFG revenue models include a negotiated distributor margin, software service fees, installation and training support or clinical-workflow integration. All are subject to Mediwhale agreement, territory rights and local healthcare rules.

5. What Mediwhale gains

  • Access to suitable new clinical and institutional channels where existing partner rights allow.
  • Incremental commercial use of retinal AI screening software and associated services.
  • Potential independently collected longitudinal data useful for research, subject to consent and data agreements.
  • Opportunities for peer-reviewed collaborative research that remains methodologically independent of commercial claims.

FFG should demonstrate actual participating institutions, country capabilities and commercial terms rather than simply promising access to an international network.

Non-negotiable distinction: Mediwhale's regulatory status cannot confer approval on IGDS or Advanced Flow. Retinal AI should not be marketed as proof of treatment efficacy, or used to steer higher-risk patients toward an unvalidated intervention.

8. Recommended opening proposal

FFG and RAHO would like to explore how Mediwhale’s retinal AI technology can support responsible cardiovascular screening, expand international clinical access and contribute to research on emerging vascular interventions. Our aim is to create commercial value and scientifically credible evidence, while respecting the independent regulatory and clinical requirements of each technology.

Suggested next step: A strategic introductory meeting with Mediwhale's co-founders and leadership to consider a two-track exploratory agreement: commercial screening/distribution and independently reviewed clinical research.
