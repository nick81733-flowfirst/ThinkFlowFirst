1 · Founding Story

The mission did not change. The opportunity became much bigger.

AFW began with a simple founding purpose: help people gain better health through wellness. The first expression was AFW Sanctuary in Cambodia, combining residential wellness and recovery with a surrounding property-and-lifestyle concept.

The original scaling model was already designed to be flexible: owned flagships where appropriate, converted third-party hotels or resorts, operating partnerships, and lifestyle real estate around selected destinations.

AFW did not begin as a technology company. It began as a wellness vision with an asset-light path to replication.
2 · Strategic Inflection

Therapeutic technology access added a second scaling path

AFW subsequently secured access to a leading-edge therapeutic technology platform associated with the NOVO / IGDS ecosystem. Subject to the final rights framework and local regulatory requirements, the strategic intent is to commercialize through agreed markets and channels beyond the Sanctuary itself.

This created a new model through partner clinics, wellness centers, dedicated outlets, referral networks and country partners. AFW no longer needs a wellness destination in every market in order to scale.

Rights diligence remains important: territory, exclusivity, excluded markets, supply continuity, transfer pricing, technology improvements, brand/training/quality rights, data rights, performance conditions and termination mechanisms should be contractually clear.
3 · Structural Market Tailwind

Health & wellness is a long-duration consumer transformation

McKinsey's Future of Wellness research has estimated the global consumer wellness market at roughly US$1.8 trillion annually and highlights stronger consumer focus on proactive health, effective and science-backed solutions, personalization and measurable outcomes.

AFW is positioning inside a structural shift from reactive care alone toward prevention, optimization, longevity and consumer-directed wellness.

14 · Cambodia Commercial

Commercial growth beyond room capacity

Long-term target: 100 new customers/month, each taking a 20-infusion package at US$150 net per infusion.

US$3.6MIllustrative annual revenue.
US$1.68MIllustrative EBITDA.
20%Operating overhead assumption.

Planning economics: 20 infusions × US$150 = US$3,000 per customer; Cambodia serves as its own country master; outlet transfer approximately US$75; underlying COGS approximately US$25 in the Cambodia model. Ramp planning starts at 5 new customers/month for 10 months, then 10/month until the longer-term target is reached.

10 outlets × 10 new customers/month = 100 new customers/month. A distributed network can scale without one giant facility.
15 · Global Platform

Capital-light replication through Exclusive Country Master Partners

AFW / Flow First Global can focus on technology access, central supply, standards, training, brand, systems, partner enablement and demand support, while local partners fund much of the outlet infrastructure and operations.

~US$20Target scaled COGS.
US$50Illustrative transfer price to the country master outside Cambodia.
US$75Illustrative downstream outlet transfer price, leaving ~US$25 country-master margin.

Illustrative mature platform at 1,000 new customers/month × 20 infusions/customer = approximately US$12M annual AFW platform revenue and approximately US$4.8M platform contribution before tax and financing.

Country architecture: any market-access fee is charged once for the country/territory to the Exclusive Country Master Partner and should be calibrated to market potential. There is currently no intention for AFW International/Global to charge an outlet-access fee; outlet rollout and outlet-level commercial terms are left to the ECMP. The US$120K Indonesia figure is a benchmark for setting up an IV-lounge-style outlet, not a country access fee.
