Hook Discovery & Creative Testing

How Think Flow First learns from effective advertising, turns one strong hook into multiple creative formats, and tests for qualified conversations rather than views alone.

Operating decision: This is a companion to the existing 29-family Master Hook Library, not a replacement. New example hooks are proposed tests only; no new wording is automatically locked or approved.

01 · Executive decision

What the team should do

Maintain the existing 29-family Master Hook Library as the canonical source of approved and locked wording. Use this companion framework for discovery, format production, experiments, scorecards and promotion of validated winners. Every proposed line is a draft until reviewed.

02 · What is new vs already covered

Family ≠ execution ≠ format

Existing families include Missing Piece (07), Decision Point (02), Overseas Treatment (03), Stroke Recovery (11), Companion Therapy (21), Sharing (23) and Technology-Led Discovery (22). Our new material is primarily new executions, seven persuasion mechanisms, a production format system and a measurement process. Do not create duplicate families simply because a wording variant sounds novel.

03 · Real-world patterns

What successful ads teach

Pattern interrupt: question an assumption. Curiosity gap: reveal a compelling question but do not hide material facts. Decision moment: reach people before a significant choice. Identity: speak to people who identify with longevity, aesthetics or biohacking. Human emotion: dignity, daily life and caregivers. Social sharing: give someone a reason to pass along useful education. Repeated identity: make Think Flow First recognisable at a glance. These are creative hypotheses, not evidence that any particular TFF hook converts.

04 · Creative-format rule

Hooks should NOT be only static or only video

Start with compelling written hooks. Express promising lines as (A) plain text/status posts, (B) high-contrast text cards, (C) realistic photograph with short caption, (D) 6–15-second captioned video, (E) 15–30-second educator talking-head video, and (F) 3–5-card educational carousel. Use the same opening line across formats wherever practical, so the test measures format, not unrelated copy changes.

05 · TFF distinctive creative system

Signature question series

Create a recurring editorial format: small heading ‘A THINK FLOW FIRST QUESTION’, one large readable question, brand lockup based on approved original logo assets, and one clear compliant action. Make it phone-first: generous type sizes, high contrast, safe margins, minimal words, subtitles on video and realistic Asian faces where people appear. Never redraw or alter the approved logo.

06 · Candidate hooks

Fresh lines to test

What if we have been asking the wrong question? / You know your blood pressure. How often do you think about circulation? / You track your sleep. You track your steps. What about flow? / The stroke may be over. The questions about recovery aren't. / Planning to travel for treatment? There is one more thing worth researching. / You may not need this conversation today. Someone you know might. / Into stem cells or longevity science? Add circulation to the conversation. / What if better health begins with a different question? / Medicine advances. So should the questions we ask. / What's the point of living longer if we forget to live well?

07 · 30-day pilot

Small, measured, manual-first

Week 1: choose 10 hooks across 5 audience interests, create an evidence/claims review, assign owner and CTA. Week 2: launch paired static/text variants with consistent audience and budgets. Week 3: turn leading hooks into short captioned videos and compare like for like. Week 4: review qualified conversations, share rate, cost per qualified conversation, lead quality and moderation outcomes. Suggested initial creative-production allocation: 40% text-led static, 25% photo + hook, 25% short video, 10% carousel. This is a proposed effort mix, not an industry benchmark.

08 · Funnel and measurement

Conversations, not vanity metrics

Primary KPI: qualified conversations and cost per qualified conversation (CPQC). Secondary: meaningful comments, shares, saves, qualified-to-consultation rate, and ad/policy rejections. Track hook ID, family, audience, market, channel, format, opening copy, CTA, organic/paid status, spend, reach, qualified enquiries and next action. Start with manual comment/DM follow-up; consider automation only when volume justifies it. A comment CTA is not a regulatory bypass.

09 · Governance

Medical and advertising safeguards

No guaranteed cures, secret-cure framing, unsupported mechanism or clinical efficacy claims, invented testimonials, fear tactics, or claims that Advanced Flow replaces established stroke care. Stroke warning signs require immediate emergency assessment. Present IGDS as the underlying technology and Advanced Flow as the patient-facing terminology; do not use NOVO on Think Flow First materials. Check the complete ad-to-DM-to-site journey against local law and each platform's rules. Publishing is not clinical approval.

10 · Decision and publishing flow

How hooks enter the master

Status: IDEA → REVIEW → APPROVED FOR TEST → TESTED → WINNER / RETIRE. Only reviewed lines get added to the Master Hook Library, preferably beneath an existing family. Locked originals stay unchanged. Record test evidence and dates. Use Nicholas's existing GitHub/Render workflow, publish deliberately with a manual Render deploy, then verify public pages. Do not rely on an automatic deployment for the requested publish step.

11 · Field checklist

One-page launch checklist

□ Confirm the audience and family's existing library entry.
□ Check accuracy and clinical/legal advertising suitability.
□ Choose one hook, one objective and one conversation CTA.
□ Make text-card and photo versions; video only where justified.
□ Set manual response ownership and response expectations.
□ Tag creative variant and measure qualified enquiries.
□ Record learnings; promote winners into the master only after review.

12 · External research prompts

Sources to revisit

TikTok Creative Center · Meta Ad Library · World Stroke Organization. Use these as inspiration and current competitive research, not as proof that a particular creative is successful. Independently check platform policies and any comparative performance claims before external presentation.
