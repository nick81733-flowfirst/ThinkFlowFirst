FlowFirst Demand Acquisition Framework

A phased system for acquiring demand without overcommitting capital early, while keeping the FlowFirst funnel, customer relationship, data and conversion system under FlowFirst control.

Core principle: FlowFirst owns the funnel. Partners help fill the funnel. We start by buying outcomes, then selectively fund and eventually own what proves itself.

1. Ownership of the funnel

The clean default boundary is that acquisition partners are responsible for creating awareness and response. Once a genuine prospect enters the FlowFirst environment, FlowFirst takes over.

Stage 1 — AudiencePartner finds or reaches the right people.
Stage 2 — ResponseProspect clicks, messages, scans, calls or submits interest.
Stage 3 — EnquiryFlowFirst receives contact details and reason for enquiry.
Stage 4 — QualificationFlowFirst determines whether the prospect fits agreed criteria.
Stage 5 — AssessmentFlowFirst speaks with the patient or family.
Stage 6 — PathwayFlowFirst recommends an appropriate next step or provider.
Stage 7 — CustomerFlowFirst manages conversion and the ongoing relationship.

Exception: trusted specialist partners may perform agreed pre-qualification or booking, but FlowFirst should still control the central CRM, attribution and final customer record.

2. Acquisition Model Phases

These phases describe how FlowFirst acquires demand over time. They are different from the implementation phases later in this document.

Acquisition Phase 1

Performance-Based Acquisition

Start here. Partners use their own resources and methods. FlowFirst pays for agreed outcomes such as valid enquiries, qualified leads or attended assessments.

Acquisition Phase 2

Strategic Channel Partnerships

Build deeper distribution relationships with rehab groups, healthcare networks, home-care providers, patient communities, associations, media and other organizations already close to the audience.

Acquisition Phase 3

Selective Funded Acquisition

Where evidence supports it and funds permit, FlowFirst selectively funds Meta, Google, creators, Telegram, seminars, content and other proven acquisition channels.

Acquisition Phase 4

FlowFirst-Owned Acquisition

Bring consistently successful channels partly or fully in-house once their economics are understood and ownership provides better economics or control.

Acquisition sequence: Buy outcomes → build strategic channels → fund what proves itself → own what becomes core.

3. Phase 1: Performance-Based Acquisition

This is the planned first live acquisition model because it reduces unnecessary upfront spend. Instead of giving an agency a budget and hoping for results, FlowFirst asks partners what result they can deliver and at what price.

Payment eventWhat it meansWhen useful
Valid EnquiryReal, non-duplicate, stroke-related prospect with valid contact details and consent.Best starting point when FlowFirst controls qualification.
Qualified LeadProspect also meets agreed qualification criteria.Useful when criteria are objective and trust/tracking are strong.

Partners may use their own compliant mix of Meta, Google, TikTok, Telegram, creators, communities, offline outreach, referral networks or other methods. FlowFirst does not need to prescribe the channel if the agreed outcome is delivered ethically and transparently.

4. Lead submission and source attribution

The preferred system is for prospects to enter a FlowFirst-controlled landing page, form or messaging route with a unique partner source code.

Example: thinkflowfirst.com/stroke?ref=PARTNER01

  • Unique landing-page link for each partner.
  • Unique QR code for offline partners, events, clinics and printed material.
  • Partner-specific WhatsApp/Telegram entry link where practical.
  • CRM automatically records source code, date/time, contact details, consent and duplicate status.
A billable lead should enter FlowFirst's system directly or be independently verified by FlowFirst.

5. External versus internal economics

Partners need to know the outcome definition and commercial terms. They do not need to know the maximum FlowFirst is prepared to pay or the full downstream economics.

Share externallyKeep internal

Source-code and reporting requirementsPartner ranking and kill/scale thresholds
Agreed price/payment structureLifetime value assumptions and downstream conversion economics

The preferred procurement principle is: tell partners the outcome we want; do not tell them the maximum we are willing to pay for it.

6. Implementation Playbook Phases

These phases describe what FlowFirst must build and operationalize so the acquisition system can function.

1 Build Core Infrastructure

Landing pages, partner-specific source codes, QR codes, enquiry form, consent wording, CRM/database, duplicate detection, source attribution and basic reporting.

2 Define Commercial Rules

Define valid enquiry, qualified lead, attended assessment, duplicates, invalid leads, geographic criteria, consent, rejection reasons, acceptance window, payment cycle and dispute process.

3 Create the Partner Program

Prepare partner brief, application process, approved messaging, claims/compliance guide, creative assets, source codes, onboarding and commercial terms.

4 Recruit the First Partner Cohort

Recruit different partner types rather than several versions of the same agency: lead-generation specialists, performance marketers, community operators, creators, referral-network builders and local market operators.

5 Launch Acquisition Phase 1

Partners begin sending leads under the performance-based model. FlowFirst receives, verifies, accepts/rejects, follows up, qualifies and records outcomes.

6 Measure the Full Funnel

Internally track Response → Valid Enquiry → Qualified Lead → Assessment → Customer → Revenue for every source, even if the partner is only paid for an earlier-stage event.

7 Optimize and Rank Partners

Classify partners as Scale, Continue, Modify or Stop based on lead quality, cost efficiency, conversion, compliance and reliability.

8 Activate Strategic Channel Partnerships

Develop deeper relationships with rehab organizations, healthcare networks, home-care providers, patient communities, associations and media platforms.

9 Activate Selective Funded Acquisition

Where evidence supports it and funds permit, FlowFirst allocates capital to promising channels to accelerate learning and scale.

10 Build FlowFirst-Owned Capability

Internalize proven, strategically important channels when doing so improves economics, control or speed.

11 Document the Playbook

Capture audiences, hooks, sources, partner types, lead benchmarks, objections, drop-off reasons, follow-up sequences and acquisition economics.

12 Replicate Market by Market

Reuse the operating system across Cambodia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Indonesia, Thailand and other markets while allowing local acquisition channels to differ.

  • Implement source-code attribution and duplicate detection.
  • Define the lead acceptance/rejection workflow.

  • Prepare approved campaign assets and claims/compliance guardrails.
  • Prepare a short partner onboarding pack and partner application form.
  • Recruit a diverse first cohort and test market pricing without disclosing FlowFirst's internal CAC ceiling.

8. What success looks like

The objective is not simply to generate a large volume of leads. FlowFirst wants to identify repeatable, measurable and economically attractive sources of genuine demand, while learning who responds, who converts, what messages work and which partners deserve more capital.

FlowFirst should become increasingly good at deciding where to buy demand, where to build partnerships, where to invest, and what to own.
