Different therapies. Different jobs. Where does Advanced Flow fit?

1. The Longevity World Is Getting Crowded

Today, longevity and regenerative clinics offer an increasingly wide range of therapies: vitamin drips, NAD+, peptides, PRP, exosomes, stem cells, and now Advanced Flow.

What is the difference — and do I have to choose one?

3. Vitamin Drips — Give the Body Ingredients

Vitamin IVs deliver vitamins, minerals and other nutrients directly into the bloodstream. Depending on the formulation, this may include vitamin C, B vitamins, magnesium and other nutrients.

Nutritional approach: “Does the body have the nutrients it needs?”

Supplying nutrients is not the same biological intervention as changing signalling, concentrating repair factors or introducing regenerative cells.

4. NAD+ — Support Cellular Metabolism

NAD+ — nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — is naturally present in our cells and plays important roles in cellular energy metabolism, mitochondrial function, DNA repair and cellular stress responses.

Metabolic approach: “Can we support the cellular machinery involved in energy and repair?”

The biological importance of NAD+ is well established. The evidence for intravenous NAD+ specifically as a routine longevity treatment remains less mature than the underlying biology.

5. Peptides — Send More Specific Instructions

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. Many naturally occurring peptides act as biological messengers and can interact with receptors or biological pathways.

Targeted signalling approach: “Can we send a more specific biological instruction?”

Different peptides may have completely different actions, so “peptide therapy” is not one treatment but a family of interventions.

6. PRP — Use Your Own Repair Signals

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma. A small amount of the patient's own blood is collected and processed to concentrate platelets, which contain growth factors and other biologically active substances involved in healing and tissue repair.

Autologous repair / growth-factor approach: “Can we concentrate the body's own repair signals and place them where they are needed?”

Autologous simply means it comes from your own body.

7. Exosomes — Communication Between Cells

Cells communicate with one another. Exosomes are tiny extracellular vesicles that can transport biological material between cells.

Cellular communication approach: “Can biological messages influence how cells respond?”

Exosome biology is an important scientific field. Commercially marketed exosome treatments should still be evaluated separately from the broader science.

8. Stem Cells — Introduce Living Cells

Stem cells can, depending on their type, renew themselves and develop into other cell types. Stem-cell therapy therefore moves into a different category altogether.

Cellular / regenerative approach: “Can living cells contribute to repair or regeneration?”

Some stem-cell applications are well established in medicine. Others remain at very different stages of clinical evidence.

9. And Then There Is Advanced Flow

Advanced Flow does not primarily provide vitamins, NAD+, peptides, platelets, exosomes or new cells.

Instead, Advanced Flow introduces another way of thinking: the physiological environment.

The question becomes: “What environment are we asking all these therapies — and our own cells — to work in?”

Advanced Flow focuses on supporting that internal physiological environment, placing it conceptually in a different category from nutrient, signalling and cellular therapies.

12. Maybe We Are Asking the Wrong Question

People often ask: “Which treatment is best?” NAD+? PRP? Stem cells? Exosomes? Advanced Flow?

Perhaps a better question is: “What job are we asking each therapy to do?”

A stem cell is not a vitamin. A peptide is not a platelet. An exosome is not NAD+. And Advanced Flow is not another stem-cell treatment. They are different biological strategies.
