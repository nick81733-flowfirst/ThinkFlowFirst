Section 01

1. Our new direction

Dear KB,

Following our recent discussions, I believe we have an opportunity to rethink how we build Flow First Malaysia (FFM).

Rather than following a traditional distribution model involving country master distributors, area distributors and multiple layers of commissions, I propose that we develop FFM into a centrally coordinated national business supported by a network of clinical and business development partners.

Our objective is a sustainable, scalable and profitable Malaysia operation capable of becoming a model for Flow First Global (FFG).

We already have promising relationships across Malaysia. Each party has different strengths, resources, networks and interests. Instead of choosing one partner over another, why not find a way to make partners out of all our potential partners?

That does not mean making everyone a shareholder. It means defining meaningful commercial roles, rewarding verified contribution and allowing everyone to benefit from collective growth.

One coordinated ecosystem — not competing distribution channels.

Section 02

2. KB's proposed new role

I see your role as considerably broader than helping us establish one Kuala Lumpur treatment center. You are positioned to commit full-time to FFM while most prospective partners have established practices or businesses and can contribute only part-time.

I propose the working title National Managing Partner / Business Development Lead, FFM, subject to agreement on the formal role, authority and responsibilities.

  1. National network development: develop and coordinate approximately 20–30 suitable clinics.
  2. Recruitment and relationships: convert existing contacts into productive partnerships, identify new clinics, negotiate and support agreements.
  3. Commercial development: work with FFG on demand creation, partner marketing, lead follow-through and local clinic activity.
  4. Operational coordination: establish supply, purchase orders, inventory, payment, logistics, reporting and service standards.
  5. Clinic activation: ensure signed clinics actually begin suitable services and develop ongoing purchasing volume.
  6. Business performance: own customer-acquisition, margin, operating-expense, purchasing and profitability KPIs.

Long-term participation and ownership

KB's full-time operating commitment should be recognized in a separately negotiated combination of management compensation, performance incentives and possible vesting-based equity. Equity should correspond to measurable value creation and ongoing obligations, not an automatic grant merely for holding a title.

Outside investors and JVs contributing capital should ordinarily purchase equity at an agreed valuation. Investor ownership and KB's earned operating equity are distinct questions. The legal entity, contributions, vesting, dilution, departures, decision rights and governance remain open.

Clinical assessment, product suitability, treatment decisions, medical supervision and administration remain with qualified and authorized clinical professionals — not FFM commercial management.

Section 04

4. One national clinic network

RAHO supply→FFG→FFM→Partner clinics→Appropriate patient services

FFG supplies FFM. FFM contracts directly with every participating clinic. Development partners support the network without creating separate wholesale reselling tiers.

  • Uniform commercial standards and clinic wholesale arrangements.
  • Direct visibility of purchasing, replenishment and network performance.
  • Room for additional clinics in any region without distributor territorial barriers.
  • Central controls for pricing, partner terms, payments, compliance and reporting.
  • Better long-term enterprise value in a coordinated national commercial business.

We should seek 20–30 operating clinics, not simply signatures. Each clinic must be assessed for legal eligibility, medical governance, staff competency, appropriate IV facilities, product handling and willingness to meet network standards.

5. Clinic Development Partner (CDP) model

Use one class of Clinic Development Partner, with no additional national/area/sub-distributor compensation layers. The CDP introduces suitable clinics, helps bring them into operation and continues to develop their business.

Proposed CDP compensationRM10per qualifying purchased infusion unit
Payment timing1 POin arrears, after subsequent paid order
Additional FFM budget?RM0funded from existing partner incentives pool
  • Credit is attached to a clinic, under a signed agreement and attribution rules.
  • Compensation is triggered by genuine, paid qualifying infusion-unit purchases by that clinic, not by a patient's treatment completion.
  • Commission on order 1 becomes payable on a qualifying paid order 2; order 2's commission becomes payable on order 3, and so forth, subject to the agreed contract and reconciliation.
  • Returns, refunds, cancelled or reversed purchases must generate credits, clawbacks or offsets.
  • Ongoing compensation reflects actual continuing clinic development and agreed services; there is no unconditional automatic lifetime entitlement.
  • Contract rules must cover attribution disputes, inactivity, ongoing work, termination, unpaid final orders, reporting and auditability.
Compliance condition: do not implement this structure as payment for prescribing, patient referrals or clinical decisions. Any healthcare-professional involvement or related-party payments require independent Malaysian legal and medical-ethics review.

Earlier clinical cost planning suggested approximately RM70 direct cost per infusion in an appropriately staffed existing clinic (roughly RM50 nursing/disposables plus RM20 equivalent medical consultation). At that level the RM160 clinical allocation could provide approximately RM90 contribution before other operating expenses, but this is not a guarantee and must be tested clinic by clinic.

The RM240 acquisition allowance is not automatically a clinic referral fee. It is a planning capacity for compliant customer acquisition, including the Think Flow First central funnel. Section 10

10. Building demand at two levels

FFG — national and cross-country digital foundation

FFG develops the Think Flow First educational platform, creative campaigns, content and central customer-enquiry funnel. It can coordinate general public education, lead qualification and appropriate introductions to qualified participating clinics.

FFM — market activation and local clinic demand

FFM helps clinics deliver physician education, community outreach, informational meetings, local relationship development, partnership introductions, staff education and compliant on-the-ground marketing.

Partner clinics already have infrastructure, teams and relationships. We should build on those strengths rather than expect them to operate a sophisticated independent digital funnel.

Long-term acquisition economics

Paid advertising and compliant referrals may be needed at launch, but the objective is to build a brand with growing direct demand and organic word of mouth, potentially reducing the portion of retail revenues consumed by acquisition costs over time.

Stroke recovery and other unmet health needs may motivate educational enquiries, but FFM must not represent Advanced Flow as proven to prevent, treat or rehabilitate stroke or other diseases absent adequate evidence and legal authorization.

Section 11

11. Centralized commercial systems

FFM should coordinate clinic onboarding, purchase orders, supply traceability, approved prices, financial reconciliation, package records and performance reporting.

We should explore centrally managed checkout and settlement, using a suitable payment processor and legally permissible clinic disbursements.

  • Distinguish payer from actual patient.
  • Record the selling organization, serving clinic, package, unit quantity and upgrades.
  • Track clinic purchases separately from actual patient administrations.
  • Allocate marketing and CDP attribution with audit trails.
  • Manage refunded/credited orders and correction of CDP commissions.
  • Maintain clinic-held clinical records and controlled access to any shared data.

These functions may be introduced in stages. The payment processor manages money; FFM's commercial platform manages customer/package/purchasing data; licensed clinics remain custodians of clinical records and patient care.

Section 13

13. Clinical, regulatory and commercial safeguards

Before any launch, confirm the legal status of Advanced Flow and its ingredients, applicable import/supply permissions, the specific clinic's authority to administer it and professional responsibility for patient assessment.

Each participating clinic must establish qualified supervision, facility suitability, informed consent, quality and traceability procedures, adverse-event escalation, documentation and product storage or preparation controls as applicable.

Marketing and patient information must remain evidence-based and compliant. We should not equate commercial interest with regulatory authorization or substantiated clinical efficacy.

CDP compensation and other partner arrangements need Malaysian legal and medical-ethics review, especially where a doctor or doctor-controlled entity is involved. Fees must correspond to genuine permitted services and must not incentivize prescribing or patient referrals in prohibited ways.

Protect personal and medical data and obtain appropriate consent and access controls. The public review page should never be used to share identifiable patient data.

15. Closing — what we are building

KB,

I believe we can build something much larger than a single center or a traditional distributor business.

FFG provides the supply platform, brand, education and international scalability. FFM develops the Malaysian commercial network and coordinates relationships with clinics. Clinics contribute existing facilities, clinicians, local credibility and suitable patient-service capacity. Partners contribute business relationships, knowledge, regional presence and continuing network development.

Your proposed role is to bring these different strengths together into a coordinated national operation.

We do not need every partner to do everything. We need every partner to contribute what they do best.

If we align interests, create demand and demonstrate a repeatable, responsible clinic business, FFM can become an important contributor to FFG's wider international strategy.

That is the direction I would like us to develop together.
