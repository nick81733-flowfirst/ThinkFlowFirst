Malaysia ECMP & Independent Clinic Network

Dr. Mah partnership framework • Earned distribution exclusivity • Independent doctor-entrepreneur model • FFG-owned outlet alternative

PROPOSED — NOT LOCKED

Version 18 October 2026 · Commercial discussion only · Not an offer, contract, clinical protocol or investment projection

1. Executive decision summary

Preferred direction: FFG appoints Dr. Mah (a PhD holder, not a medical doctor) as its preferred Malaysia commercial development partner. He receives exclusive responsibility for recruiting and developing independent Malaysian doctor-clinic partners from appointment, while nationwide product distribution exclusivity is earned only after measurable performance.

FFG keeps its technology, product supply, pricing control under agreed contracts and the reserved right to own and operate clinics. FFG's own outlets are a backup and strategic option, not the default national rollout. Dr. Mah and independent medical clinics take on much of the clinic management and customer-acquisition burden.

Crucial distinction: Exclusive partner-clinic recruitment from day one does not mean unconditional Malaysia-wide product exclusivity from day one.

2. Why the ECMP route is attractive

Five FFG-owned eight-chair centers could earn higher operating contribution on paper, but would require centralized supervision of doctors, nurses, employment, leases, operating systems, marketing campaigns, agents/sub-agents, CRM, regulatory matters, and potentially home-service nurse transport and scheduling. Acquisition cost is presently an untested assumption.

By contrast, 10–20 independent doctor-entrepreneurs may already own premises, have appropriate existing teams and possess established patient relationships. Each carries its own operating and lawful customer-development responsibilities. This distributes commercial execution risk, although it does not eliminate shared regulatory, product-safety or demand risk.

7. Two levels of exclusivity — intended policy

A. Immediate exclusive partner-clinic development right

  • During the agreed appointment, Dr. Mah has exclusive responsibility for commercial recruitment and appointment of independent Malaysian third-party partner clinics.
  • FFG does not bypass him by signing third-party doctor-clinic outlets directly and forwards qualifying unsolicited commercial clinic inquiries to his organization.
  • FFG can contact doctors for legitimate scientific, regulatory, product-safety and professional education purposes; these are not disguised clinic recruitment.
  • FFG may establish and operate its own controlled outlets, including hiring or contracting suitably licensed clinical staff. This must not be a pretext for recruiting outside third-party partners.
  • Recruitment exclusivity has agreed duration, performance reviews, notice/remediation provisions, termination rights and applicable competition-law review.

B. Later earned national distribution exclusivity

Nationwide exclusive master-distribution rights are not automatically granted. They become available only after confirmed, sustained performance and written agreement on territory, channels, FFG-owned clinics, network oversight, safety obligations and continuing minimum purchases.

9. Allocation of responsibilities

  • FFG: product supply, manufacturing quality and traceability, permitted scientific/product support, contractual oversight, protection of technology and brand, applicable regulatory obligations.
  • Dr. Mah / ECMP company: recruit and support qualified independent clinic businesses; lawful distribution, logistics, local administration, commercial training, inventory/collections, and network performance reporting.
  • Independent medical clinic operators: their own legal registration, qualified personnel, clinical decisions, consent, patient records, insurance, clinical governance, safe administration, approved marketing, and any necessary emergency arrangements.
  • Customer acquisition: predominantly decentralized to independent clinic businesses, supported by ECMP's lawful commercial tools. Social media, lead handling, CRM, agents and sub-agents may require considerable management. No payment arrangements may improperly influence medical referrals or clinical decisions.
  • Home infusion: separate scope requiring dedicated clinical, legal, insurance, transport, nursing and economic validation before inclusion.

10. Rights over FFG-owned outlets

FFG's ability to develop company-controlled clinics remains reserved. Dr. Mah does not automatically collect RM80 on sales made by those outlets. If he later earns national exclusivity, the parties may place FFG outlets under an agreed commercial distribution umbrella, with an explicit service schedule and negotiated margin treatment. Ownership and management of FFG-controlled businesses do not automatically transfer.

11. Regulatory and evidence gates

Before clinical commercialization: Independent Malaysian advice must establish product classification, applicable NPRA/MOH approvals, import/supply pathway, clinic licensing, insurance, advertising permission, professional conduct and quality obligations. The commercial target of 20 infusions per customer does not establish an approved or medically appropriate treatment protocol.

Advanced Flow is not established in this framework as an effective stroke treatment. Stroke and other disease-specific claims require appropriate clinical evidence and authorization. Neither existing patient relationships nor commercial sales targets should influence independent clinicians' treatment decisions. Contracts must avoid patient-referral fees, improper prescribing incentives and other prohibited practices. Competition-law review is required for the proposed exclusive recruitment and distribution arrangements.

12. Decisions to discuss before locking

  1. Confirm the legal entity to serve as Dr. Mah's ECMP and the true responsibilities funded by the RM80 spread.
  2. Agree the exact definition and limits of exclusive partner-clinic recruitment, and reserved FFG scientific and owned-outlet rights.
  3. Agree duration, quarterly checkpoints, milestones, recovery rights, and whether regional rights precede national exclusivity.
  4. Approve payment security, taxes, shipping, inventory commitments, stock ownership, credit terms and quality/recall obligations.
  5. Decide how FFG-owned outlets are treated after earned exclusivity and whether any margin is payable for defined services.
  6. Validate the clinics' real staffing, delivery, acquisition and compliance costs and the 20-infusion lifetime assumption with pilot data.
  7. Establish the lawful clinical and regulatory pathway, especially for any stroke-related use, before authorizing promotion or treatment.
  8. Determine home-service scope separately, including transport, nursing, safety and pricing.
Proposed stakeholder decision: Endorse the ECMP-led network as the preferred Malaysia development strategy, accept Dr. Mah's initial exclusive third-party clinic-recruitment role in principle, preserve FFG-owned outlet rights, and negotiate earned—not automatic—national distribution exclusivity. No numerical or legal terms are locked by publication of this draft.
