AFW Holdings
Alternative Fundraising & Capital Architecture

A framework for funding each economic engine with the capital source best suited to it — while AFW preserves control of the assets, rights and long-term upside that matter most.

Separate discussion framework · Does not replace the current AFW Holdings investor deck or existing fundraising plan

1. Why Reconsider a Single HoldCo Raise?

The original approach asks one investor to invest into AFW Holdings and indirectly fund multiple needs across very different businesses. The alternative is to let each engine attract the type of capital best suited to that engine.

AFW may not need to sell HoldCo equity to finance every operating business, property obligation or country expansion.

This matters because AFW is currently operating with lean financial and human resources. The group has several monetizable assets and opportunities, but insufficient management bandwidth and capital to activate all of them simultaneously.

The alternative architecture is designed to mobilize capital around AFW while minimizing dilution of the strategic core.

2. The Core Principle

Control the hard assetsPropertyCo retains control of land, titles, buildings and strategic development rights.
Control the international railsInternational retains Advanced Flow access, international commercial rights, country architecture and the multi-door demand engine.
Protect the long-term science upsideBiosciences remains dependent on the RAHO–FFG rights framework and is not independently monetized for now.

AFW does not necessarily need majority ownership of every operating company. It needs to retain control of the assets and rights that those businesses depend on.

4. PropertyCo — Refinance, Sell, Reinvest

PropertyCo should not automatically raise equity simply because debt exists. The preferred strategy is to make the debt more manageable while turning property sales into an active cash-generation engine.

Preferred refinanceSeek roughly US$600k at a lower interest rate and a substantially longer tenor — potentially around 15 years if available.
Fallback structureRetain the existing US$338k loan and raise only about US$250k if refinancing is not available on acceptable terms.

Existing monetizable inventory

AFW currently has 55 lifestyle plots remaining at approximately US$30,000 per plot, representing about US$1.65m of headline sales inventory.

5. Sanctuary OpCo — Capital-Light, Strategically Flexible

The Sanctuary's physical base already exists. The Phase 1 equipment/setup plan is approximately US$17k using China-direct procurement. The larger need is management depth, demand generation and working capital rather than heavy capex.

Property and operations should remain separated. Sanctuary OpCo pays PropertyCo:

  • US$6,000/month facility rent/capital charge; and
  • 4% of Sanctuary revenue.

Sanctuary can therefore accept a strategic operator or investor — potentially even a majority investor if compelling — without AFW giving away the underlying land.

Strategic rationale: AFW can potentially earn from Sanctuary through retained equity, PropertyCo rent/revenue participation and International Advanced Flow supply economics even if an outside operator owns a large share of Sanctuary OpCo.

6. Cambodia Commercial — Fund the Local Business Locally

Cambodia Commercial can attract a local strategic investor after sufficient proof, without giving that investor International rights or ownership of the broader AFW ecosystem.

The mature planning model already developed is US$3.6m annual revenue / US$1.68m EBITDA, based on 100 new customers per month and 20% operating overhead. These are target economics, not a present valuation basis.

An indicative US$250k–300k capitalization could fund local market activation, partner development, staffing, demand generation and working capital.

At the mature target, Cambodia would purchase approximately US$1.2m of product annually from International, preserving upstream economics even if AFW eventually owns less of the Cambodia operating company.

11. The Main Trade-Off

The alternative structure is more capital-efficient, but more operationally demanding. It requires multiple financing, partnership and execution workstreams rather than one fundraising transaction. AFW's current lean management setup is therefore the largest practical constraint.

The solution is not necessarily a large C-suite. It is clear ownership of the revenue number for each major workstream:

  • Property commercialization
  • Sanctuary operations
  • Cambodia commercialization
  • International country partnerships

Finance, legal/contracts, branding, CRM/data, investor relations and parts of demand generation can remain shared centrally.

12. Recommended Sequence

  1. PropertyCo: pursue refinancing while immediately accelerating plot/property sales.
  2. Sanctuary: complete the lean setup, strengthen management and prove occupancy/revenue traction; keep a strategic operator option open.
  3. Cambodia Commercial: prove customer acquisition and partner economics, then invite local strategic capital.
  4. International: recruit the first 2–3 ECMPs, prove the country template, then scale to the wider first-wave market list.
  5. Biosciences: leave unchanged pending clarity on the RAHO–FFG agreement and rights structure.
  6. HoldCo: raise only minimal capital unless a genuinely strategic investor can materially strengthen the group.

13. Capital Priority — Least Dilutive First

  1. Property sales
  2. Debt refinancing
  3. Country market-access fees
  4. Opening inventory cash
  5. Operating cash flow
  6. Strategic subsidiary-level equity
  7. AFW HoldCo equity last
The objective is not to avoid investors. It is to use the right investor, at the right level, for the right reason.

14. Strategic Conclusion

The alternative architecture suggests that AFW's immediate challenge may not simply be a shortage of capital. It is also a shortage of management and commercialization resources needed to activate the assets and opportunities already present.

Instead of selling a meaningful percentage of the entire AFW ecosystem to fund every need, AFW can potentially:

  • refinance PropertyCo and turn property sales into an active cash engine;
  • allow Sanctuary and Cambodia Commercial to attract their own strategic operators or investors;
  • use ECMP capital to fund country-by-country international rollout;
  • keep HoldCo capital requirements minimal; and
  • preserve control of Property, International and Biosciences.
Why this may be the better path: it mobilizes more forms of capital, reduces unnecessary HoldCo dilution, matches investors to the engines they understand, and reserves the most valuable AFW equity for partners who can contribute more than money.
