Discussion Paper · 30 September 2026

Commercialisation Proposal — Discussion Review

Management response to the consultant’s Commercialisation Proposal Draft 2. The purpose is not to reject the proposal, but to identify what should be retained, what needs revision, and what the next draft should answer.

Prepared for discussion with management and marketing consultants · Standalone document

Executive view

The proposal contains useful building blocks, especially Cambodia market mapping, regulatory awareness, CRM, operating roles and a phased sales roadmap. The main concern is strategic focus: the draft currently reads more like a plan to distribute nanobubble infusions through clinics than a plan to build FlowFirst as a scalable commercial platform.

The next draft should answer one central question: what is the fastest, safest and most capital-efficient way for FlowFirst to prove a repeatable commercial model in Cambodia before scaling regionally?

Our preferred architecture is now clear: ThinkFlowFirst creates education and demand; FlowCare handles navigation, coordination, referral and follow-up; independent providers and FlowFirst Centers deliver appropriate services; AFW Sanctuary is the residential/destination recovery platform; NOVO/IGDS is an enabling technology within the system rather than the entire consumer proposition.

Page-by-page management comments

Agree — elevate

Page 1 — Cambodia medical and stroke market

The stroke burden data is useful and should not remain background material. It supports making stroke and neurological recovery one of the principal commercial entry markets.

Requested change: move stroke from “market information” into the core commercial thesis and identify the first priority patient and partner segments.

Source: consultant draft, page 1.

Change

Page 1 — Hospitals, rehabilitation and home-care providers

The provider list is useful, but it should become a prioritised partner map rather than a directory.

Requested change: classify each organisation as acute hospital, specialist, rehabilitation provider, home-care provider, referral source, delivery partner or AFW feeder. Then identify the first 10 organisations to approach, in order, with reasons.

Source: consultant draft, pages 1–2.

Change importance

Pages 2–3 — Aesthetics market

We agree there is an existing commercial opportunity in aesthetics and IV wellness. However, aesthetics should be treated as an adjacent cash-flow and market-testing channel, not as a brand-defining pillar equal to medical recovery.

Reason: FlowFirst needs long-term credibility across stroke recovery, cardiovascular health, longevity, rehabilitation and advanced recovery. Early association mainly with “glow drips” or beauty IVs could narrow the brand unnecessarily.

Source: consultant draft, pages 2–3.

Change

Page 3 — Target market

The 22–45, predominantly female aesthetic profile may be valid for that channel, but it should not become the master FlowFirst customer profile.

Requested change: create separate personas for stroke recovery families/caregivers, longevity customers, aesthetic/wellness customers, overseas-treatment decision makers and professional/clinical partners.

Source: consultant draft, page 3.

Expand

Page 4 — Marketing levers

Clinic education, treatment bundling and package pricing are useful for partners, but this is mainly a product-selling model. The next draft should also show how FlowFirst itself creates demand.

Requested model: ThinkFlowFirst → enquiry → FlowCare → appropriate partner/FlowFirst Center/AFW → treatment or program → follow-up and outcomes.

Source: consultant draft, page 4.

Agree

Page 4 — Regulatory checklist

This is one of the stronger parts of the proposal. Facility licensing, responsible doctors, product status and importer/distributor responsibility must be treated as part of commercialization from the start.

Requested change: convert the checklist into a responsibility matrix showing what FlowFirst, importer, clinic and doctor are each responsible for: importation, storage, administration, consent, documentation and incident reporting.

Source: consultant draft, page 4.

Question

Page 4 — Shareholding

The proposed 50% / 30% / 20% shareholding should not simply appear inside an organisation chart without showing the underlying contribution and governance logic.

Questions: Which legal entity does this refer to? What cash, IP, assets, rights or future obligations justify each percentage? How are vesting, dilution, decision rights and non-performance handled?

Source: consultant draft, page 4.

Change governance

Page 5 — CEO role

The CEO role is generally sensible, but “final say on pricing and hires” is too broad for a founder-led company with several shareholders.

Requested change: create a delegation-of-authority matrix. Normal operating decisions can sit with management; reserved matters such as fundraising, senior hires, material pricing changes, IP transactions, debt, major capex, exclusive partnerships and country entry should require board/shareholder approval.

Source: consultant draft, page 5.

Mostly agree

Page 5 — COO role

Order fulfilment, inventory, SOPs, product traceability, complaints and incident handling belong here. “Set up clinical study” is too broad as a routine operations responsibility.

Requested wording: “Coordinate structured outcomes collection and support properly approved research where applicable.”

KPIs should measure: active partners, monthly revenue, treatments per active partner, gross margin, qualified lead conversion, repeat use and partner retention — not just number of signed accounts.

Change significantly

Page 7 — Compensation

The compensation plan appears to fit existing individuals into fixed boxes rather than start from the functions the company genuinely needs.

Requested structure: founders = equity plus modest/deferred compensation; operating staff = market salaries; sales = base plus performance commission; consultants = retainers/project fees; advisors = equity or fees only where justified.

Salary should compensate work performed and should not be disbursed according to a person’s percentage of total payroll.

Source: consultant draft, page 7.

Reject as master model

Page 8 — “Aesthetics = B2B; Medical = B2C”

This distinction is too simplistic. Medical and aesthetics can both operate through B2B2C channels.

Replace with three channel types: (1) FlowFirst-generated consumer demand, (2) partner-generated demand, and (3) residential/destination demand through AFW.

Source: consultant draft, page 8.

Useful start

Page 8 — Pricing and margins

The model is useful as a first contribution-margin estimate but is not yet a full unit-economics model.

Add: consumables, saline, nurse, doctor, logistics, wastage, partner share, customer acquisition cost, payment costs, tax, insurance/refunds and overhead allocation. Then show revenue per patient, gross profit per patient, CAC, repeat rate, lifetime value and payback period.

Source: consultant draft, page 8.

Reject

Page 8 — “Once we take on sales, don’t focus so much on comms”

For FlowFirst, communication and education are part of the commercial moat. The market does not already understand the FlowFirst philosophy or the technology. Communication should become more systematic as we scale, not less important.

Source: consultant draft, page 8.

Focus

Page 9 — Months 0–2

The first phase should be simplified around five deliverables:

  • Regulatory pathway confirmed.
  • One principal medical use case selected — preferably stroke/neurological recovery.
  • Approximately three pilot partners recruited.
  • CRM/outcomes system live.
  • ThinkFlowFirst demand-generation pilot live, with FlowCare handling navigation and follow-up.

Source: consultant draft, page 9.

Change KPIs

Page 9 — Months 3–6

Do not optimise for number of clinics signed. Optimise for active usage and economics.

Recommended measures: active partners, monthly treatments, treatments per partner, repeat orders, patient repeat use, gross margin, conversion, satisfaction and safety/incident metrics.

Source: consultant draft, page 9.

Reject wording

Page 9 — “Records to prove efficacy”

Commercial patient records do not by themselves prove efficacy.

Replace with: “Collect structured real-world outcomes data to evaluate safety, utilisation and outcome signals.”

Source: consultant draft, page 9.

Make milestone-based

Page 9 — Months 7–9

Expansion should not happen merely because month seven arrives. It should be conditional on evidence of repeat demand, viable economics, partner utilisation, compliance/safety and customer satisfaction.

Source: consultant draft, page 9.

Too much too early

Page 9 — Months 10–12

International expansion and opening another centre should not be automatic Year 1 activities. We already have AFW Sanctuary. Additional fixed infrastructure should follow demonstrated demand and inability of the partner network or AFW to meet it.

Source: consultant draft, page 9.

Priority commercial strategy for the next 6 months

1. Prove one medical market

Make stroke and neurological recovery the flagship proof market. Build a structured recovery proposition around rehabilitation, circulation-supportive therapies, medical oversight and NOVO/IGDS where permitted and appropriate. The proposition should be a FlowFirst Recovery Program, not simply a “NOVO treatment”.

2. Build the FlowFirst demand engine

ThinkFlowFirst should generate interest and education. FlowCare should qualify, navigate and follow up. The objective is to own more of the customer journey rather than depend entirely on a clinic’s own marketing.

3. Establish a compliant delivery network

Use licensed doctors, clinics, rehabilitation providers, home-service providers and FlowFirst Centers as the delivery network. FlowFirst supplies brand, demand, training, protocols, CRM, technology and patient journey support; partners provide regulated clinical delivery.

4. Use AFW Sanctuary as an existing strategic asset

AFW should not appear as a minor “other partnership.” It is the group’s existing residential/destination platform for recovery, rehabilitation and longevity and should be integrated directly into the commercialization plan.

5. Keep aesthetics as an adjacent revenue channel

Test aesthetics/wellness for early revenue, repeat use and operational learning, but do not allow it to define the FlowFirst brand.

Suggested discussion position

We are not looking merely for a plan to sell nanobubble infusions in Cambodia. We are building FlowFirst as a patient-demand, recovery and technology commercialization platform. We want the next draft to show how we prove one or two commercial models exceptionally well in Cambodia before adding channels, headcount, physical centres or new countries.
