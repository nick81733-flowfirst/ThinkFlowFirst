Agenda 01 · Global footprint

Understanding Mediwhale's international operations

10 minutes

Objective: Understand the actual operational footprint and which relationships are accessible for collaboration.

  • Which countries and product versions are currently approved or registered, and for precisely which intended uses? Can we reconcile the presentation's eight territories with more recent public descriptions?
  • How many active clinical sites use Dr. Noon CVD globally, broken down by country?
  • How many retinal cameras actively run the software, by country and in total? Are they Mediwhale-supplied or third-party devices?
  • What are actual monthly screening volumes, and what proportion of installed sites are active?
  • What is the mix of hospitals, cardiology clinics, diabetes clinics, ophthalmology providers, screening centers and wellness-related facilities?
  • Which countries are managed by Mediwhale directly, exclusive distributors or other partners?
Useful takeaway: Country-level approved-product, active-site, camera, volume and partner/channel-rights summary.

Agenda 02 · Clinical and product roadmap

Dr. Noon CVD, CVD Plus and Brain

10 minutes

Objective: Understand current validated indications, developments under investigation and any research relevance to cardiovascular and neurological health.

Existing Dr. Noon CVD

  • Which cardiovascular outcomes does the present score predict? What evidence is specific to stroke versus broader cardiovascular events?
  • Who is the validated target population, and what are important exclusions or limitations?
  • Is longitudinal use supported? Can changes in scores be interpreted clinically, or only investigated as research measures?

Dr. Noon CVD Plus (under development)

  • What does the proposed four-level cardiovascular risk stratification add over the existing CVD tool?
  • What is the validation plan, and what are the likely steps before commercial availability?

Dr. Noon Brain (under development)

  • Which conditions are targeted: cerebrovascular risk, stroke, dementia or other neurological endpoints?
  • What are the current study populations, evidence, roadmap and intended clinical workflow?

Exploratory Advanced Flow research

  • Could retinal AI measures be included as exploratory endpoints alongside conventional validated measurements in independently designed studies?
  • What would be required to test reliability, clinical meaning and any sensitivity to change over time?
  • Would Dr. James be open to a separate research-methods discussion with relevant clinical investigators?
Scientific boundary: A Dr. Noon cardiovascular risk score does not establish that Advanced Flow is indicated or effective, and a change in AI score must not be treated as proof of treatment benefit without validation.
Useful takeaway: Clinical/product roadmap plus a yes/no on a separate research feasibility discussion.

Agenda 03 · Indonesia opportunity

RAHO Club as a nationwide screening partner

15 minutes

Objective: Assess Mediwhale's interest in a potentially scalable screening partnership across Indonesia.

Working context from FFG: RAHO Club currently operates approximately 35–40 centers across Indonesia, with an ambition to reach 100 by 2027. These are reported network figures and an expansion target, not confirmed Mediwhale deployment commitments.

Could existing RAHO Club relationships provide Mediwhale with a ready-made channel for medically supervised cardiovascular risk screening?

  • Is Mediwhale interested in meeting RAHO leadership to evaluate a multi-location agreement?
  • Are Indonesian territory rights already assigned? Would Mediwhale and/or its Indonesian distributor lead the agreement?
  • Can RAHO facilities lawfully provide image acquisition and screening, or must a licensed clinical provider perform and interpret each service?
  • What cameras, software, cloud connectivity, staff training and ongoing maintenance are needed per site?
  • What are device acquisition or leasing costs, licensing fees and the cost of each AI interpretation? Are network-volume terms feasible?
  • How would clinical reporting, referrals, patient consent and data ownership be managed?
  • Could a 3–5 site pilot establish workflow, utilization, economics and clinical acceptability before wider rollout?
Useful takeaway: Mediwhale's initial interest, Indonesian channel contact and requirements for a joint RAHO feasibility discussion.

Agenda 04 · Screening access

Free or low-cost community screening

10 minutes

Objective: Establish an accessible, sustainable screening model with appropriate clinical supervision.

  • Can FFG and partners run free or subsidized screening events, beginning with a potential 100-person Cambodia pilot?
  • What would fully loaded cost per screened person be at different volumes?
  • Can the model be adapted to licensed clinical partners in Malaysia and Singapore?
  • What are the requirements for image quality, trained operators, physician review, reporting and appropriate follow-up?
  • Which marketing claims are permitted in each market, and who is responsible for the required authorizations?
Useful takeaway: Indicative pilot cost structure, operating requirements and a contact for implementation.

Agenda 05 · Education and demand creation

Think Flow First as a screening demand partner

10 minutes

Objective: Explore whether FFG's prevention campaigns can help Mediwhale's clinical sites reach more appropriate screening participants.

  • Introduce Think Flow First: stroke awareness, cardiovascular prevention, public education, digital campaigns and personal follow-up.
  • Could co-branded community initiatives help clinics organize screening days and health-education sessions?
  • Can participating sites use standardized outreach materials and an evidence-based clinical referral pathway?
  • Would Mediwhale support pilot activity with education materials, equipment access, staff training or preferential screening terms?
  • How should separate, optional consent for Think Flow First educational follow-up be handled?
Patient-first separation: Screening participants receive their results and clinically appropriate guidance regardless of any interest in FFG. Opting into Think Flow First information is voluntary. Screening results are not used to promote Advanced Flow as an indicated treatment.
Useful takeaway: Interest in a joint screening/education campaign, together with permissible claims and support options.

Agenda 06 · Reciprocal network development

Mediwhale's clinical network and potential FFG introductions

10 minutes

Objective: Explore whether selected existing clinical partners would welcome introductions to FFG for an independent evaluation of Advanced Flow.

  • Which Mediwhale-connected providers operate infusion services, preventive medicine, longevity clinics or integrative medicine practices?
  • Would Mediwhale be comfortable asking selected providers whether they want an introductory discussion with FFG?
  • Which markets might be realistic first: Cambodia, Malaysia or Singapore, then others with suitable clinical and regulatory pathways?
  • Could both parties introduce new suitable healthcare institutions to one another?
  • How would introductions, roles, conflicts of interest and commercial incentives be governed?
Boundary: No partner should be asked to offer Advanced Flow unless the applicable regulatory status, clinical evidence, quality, safety and professional obligations have been independently reviewed. Mediwhale's technology approvals do not authorize Advanced Flow.
Useful takeaway: Two or three voluntary introduction candidates and a process for further assessment.

Agenda 07 · Partnership models

Commercial and strategic structure

5 minutes

Objective: Identify a practical starting structure rather than negotiating a broad agreement prematurely.

  • Preferential screening access for FFG's participating clinical partners.
  • Co-developed campaigns and screening programs in agreed markets.
  • A proposed RAHO–Mediwhale Indonesia arrangement, subject to territory rights and RAHO agreement.
  • Reciprocal business introductions, with clear permission, responsibilities and transparent compensation if appropriate.
  • Separate research collaboration, with independently agreed protocols, ethics, data governance and publication rights.
Useful takeaway: Preferred initial workstreams, commercial decision-makers and proposed agreement route.
