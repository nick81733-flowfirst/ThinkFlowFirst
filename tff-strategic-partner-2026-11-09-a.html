Integrated Demand Creation Strategy & Master Hook Families

One consolidated strategic reference covering hook families, creative testing, entry doors, channels, conversational funnels, creators and global market intelligence.

What this consolidated appendix covers

This single appendix replaces the former Appendices A–F. It combines our Master Hook Library and taxonomy, creative testing, demand entry doors, online/offline channels, conversation-first funnel, creator and medical expert distribution, and global prelaunch / cross-border healthcare market strategy. The main briefing stays deliberately strategic; the detailed operating model and partner mandate remain separate.

29 canonical families × 5 examples = 145 hook examples. Existing phrases are reproduced from the published master library, and newly supplied examples are explicitly marked as discussion variants. These are not 145 pre-approved advertisements.

The hook architecture

Our two core audience zones are Stay Well (longevity, prevention, health optimization) and Get More Help (stroke, caregiver needs, recovery, ongoing health decisions). Stroke is the first disease-related focus, not the final market. Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, dementia, cardiovascular and other conditions could become distinct future content streams, each subject to evidence and advertising review.

Each asset should be tagged with a hook family, precise creative wording, demand entry door, target audience, language, country, channel, CTA, claim approval and measurable conversion outcomes.

Entry doors and distribution channels

A hook is the specific creative line; a demand entry door is why the individual engages; a channel is where it reaches them. The framework covers curiosity, problem recognition, decision, navigation, expert/evidence, family, social sharing, search, event, experience, referral and medical travel doors.

Compare channels across paid social, creators, clinician educators, communities, search/educational resources, events and offline healthcare partnerships. This channel × entry-door matching is a testing matrix, not proven performance. Each test should use a consistent campaign ID and measure cost per qualified conversation, consultation and purchase where lawful.

Why we prefer conversation-first

Infusion and serious-disease content can face advertising restrictions, including review of linked landing pages. We therefore currently prefer educational question → voluntary DM or comment → transparent educational response → consented follow-up → appropriate clinical or future-access pathway, rather than mandating a technical product landing page at first contact.

This is not an attempt to disguise claims or avoid advertising reviews; website content, DMs and medical travel promotion remain subject to relevant restrictions. The strategic partner should challenge this hypothesis against approved educational landing pages and hybrid journeys. Accuracy, minimal sensitive data collection, disclosure, opt-out and qualified clinical escalation remain essential.

Influencers and Campaign Studio

We envisage trusted healthcare educators, caregiver advocates, longevity creators, clinics and local partners delivering approved, localized hook families. The proposed TFF Campaign Studio would choose a hook, translate and adapt it, obtain professional/compliance approval, produce native creative and create attribution tags. Performance should be measured through qualified and commercial outcomes, not followers alone.

Creator and TFF shared inboxes, partner attribution, commissions and downstream lead ownership require clear consent, professional independence and written data-sharing rules.

Global demand generation before market entry

Our commercialization horizon is worldwide. Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines are illustrative early-market prospects, not geographic limits. We are especially interested in global concentrations of medical travel and high-value health consumers: Mexico in relation to US demand, Türkiye across Europe and the Middle East, UAE/Gulf healthcare and longevity markets, plus Europe, East Asia and other corridors. These are exploratory hypotheses rather than evidence of Advanced Flow-specific demand.

Distinguish source market (where interest originates), authorized treatment destination (where suitable care might lawfully be delivered) and future domestic commercialization market. Before a country permits local access, TFF can explore legally permissible education, voluntary opt-in audience-building and future-access interest. Medical tourism may be a conditional nearer-term path, but only with verified provider/product authorization, source-country promotion compliance and medical travel suitability.

Separate content reach, conversation, consented opt-in, qualified interest, expressed travel/future-access interest and actual customers. Demand intelligence should help FFG decide where to allocate regulatory investment and select local partners—not imply guaranteed revenue.

Testing brief and companion references

Suggested test design: select a lawful source market and priority audience; compare two hook families, two feasible channels and conversation-first versus a compliant alternative. Pre-agree approval, media budget, qualification thresholds, attribution, human follow-up and go/no-go economics. Do not publish campaigns automatically or equate responses with prospective patients.
