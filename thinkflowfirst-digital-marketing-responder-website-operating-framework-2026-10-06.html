Think Flow First Responder & Website Workflow

The practical operating system for turning curiosity-led responses into trackable, permission-based conversations through ThinkFlowFirst.com — from the original hook, to the private link, to seven questions, lead capture, routing, nurture, sharing and eventual human follow-up.

Core operating principle: social media creates curiosity and response. The private responder bridge sends the right person to the right ThinkFlowFirst.com entry door. The website educates, qualifies and captures. Human follow-up is reserved for people who ask for it or clearly need it.

1. What ThinkFlowFirst.com is for

ThinkFlowFirst.com is not primarily a public advertising landing page. It is the education and trust-building destination for people who have already responded to the Think Flow First demand funnel.

The team should therefore think of the website as the controlled middle of the funnel:

Stage 1
Acquire

Hook, post, video, partner, event or referral creates curiosity.

Stage 2
Respond

Person comments, DMs, messages, follows or asks for more.

Stage 3
Educate & qualify

Private tagged link opens the appropriate ThinkFlowFirst.com entry door and seven-question journey.

Stage 4
Capture & act

Contact details, intentions, source attribution and follow-up needs enter the lead system.

Shorthand: Acquire → Educate → Qualify → Capture → Nurture → Share → Refer → Convert.

2. The full responder workflow

  1. Publish compliant curiosity-led content. Every item should have a campaign/hook identity before publication.
  2. Wait for an engagement signal. Examples: comment, DM, WhatsApp message, Telegram message, reply to a partner, QR scan or direct request for more information.
  3. Identify why the person responded. The responder should know which hook or topic brought them in.
  4. Choose the correct website entry door. Do not send everybody to the generic homepage if their context is already known.
  5. Generate a tagged link. Use the internal responder-link generator so source, campaign, hook and delivery channel are preserved.
  6. Send a short private reply plus the link. Do not attempt to explain the whole health concept inside the DM.
  7. Visitor reads the relevant Flow First education. The site resolves the curiosity and builds trust before asking for information.
  8. Visitor completes the seven-question intake. Questions are presented one at a time, not as a long medical form.
  9. Visitor provides contact information and chooses one or more intentions. These are multi-select.
  10. The lead record is created. The record should preserve both what the visitor told us and where they originally came from.
  11. Action depends on their intentions. Help Now requires human follow-up; Country Alert adds them to the relevant availability list; Keep Posted adds them to approved educational nurture.
  12. Sharing creates another acquisition path. A useful page or guide can be forwarded to family and friends, creating secondary/referral visitors.

4. How tagged links work

Every private link should carry enough information for us to know where the person came from, which campaign/hook caused the response, and how the link was delivered.

Standard structure:

https://thinkflowfirst.com/stroke?src=tiktok&campaign=stroke-awareness&hook=STK-03&via=whatsapp
FieldMeaningExamples
srcOriginal acquisition sourcetiktok, facebook, instagram, partner, seminar, qr
campaignCampaign or initiativestroke-awareness, stemcell-companion, overseas-treatment
hookSpecific content/hook identifierSTK-03, STEM-02, OVS-04
viaChannel used to deliver the private linkwhatsapp, telegram, messenger, dm, email
Team rule: do not manually invent tracking parameters when the responder tool is available. Generate the link through the responder-link generator to reduce spelling mistakes and attribution loss.

5. What the responder should say

The responder's job is to bridge curiosity into education, not diagnose, debate or make treatment claims inside social media messaging.

Good

Short and contextual

“Thanks for reaching out. This page explains what we mean by Think Flow First in the context of stroke recovery. It also lets you tell us a little about the situation if you want a more relevant follow-up.”

Avoid

Long medical explanation in DM

Do not try to answer complex clinical questions, promise outcomes, or turn the DM into an unstructured medical consultation.

Good

Let the website do the heavy lifting

Use the correct entry door. The site should carry the deeper Flow First, RAHO, trust and evidence context.

Avoid

Untracked generic links

Do not send a bare homepage link when source and intent are already known. We lose valuable learning if attribution disappears.

6. The seven-question intake

The intake is deliberately conversational. It is not intended to imitate a hospital intake form. Its purpose is to understand enough context to provide a useful next step.

#Question areaWhy we ask
1Who are you asking about?Distinguishes self, spouse, parent, relative, friend, etc.
2What is the situation?Captures the visitor's own description of the health situation or goal.
3When did it begin / happen?Provides timeline and urgency context.
4Biggest concern or challenge now?Surfaces the practical problem they most care about.
5What are you hoping to understand or improve?Clarifies desired outcome and information need.
6What care or approaches are being used now?Shows what is already happening and helps avoid irrelevant follow-up.
7Where are you based?Supports provider routing, country availability and market demand intelligence.

After the seven questions, the visitor provides name, email and optional WhatsApp/Telegram, then chooses how they want us to stay in touch.

7. Final intentions are multi-select

The three choices are not either/or. A visitor may select one, two or all three.

Action

I'd like help now

Create an actionable lead and place it in the human follow-up queue.

Availability

Tell me when Flow First reaches my country

Add the person to the relevant country waitlist while preserving their topic and source.

Nurture

Keep me posted

Add the person to the Flow First Brief / approved educational-update list.

9. How the team acts on the data

Queue A
Help Now

Human review and follow-up. This is the priority operational queue.

Queue B
Country Waitlist

Group by country to identify demand and notify people when service becomes available.

Queue C
Keep Posted

Educational nurture, Flow First Brief and appropriate RAHO / platform updates.

Analytics
Source performance

Compare hooks, entry doors and sources by quality and eventual conversion.

One person remains one master lead record. The queues are views or actions against that record, not three separate copies of the person.

10. Attribution and learning loop

The marketing team must be able to answer more than “how many leads did we get?” The system should show the path from the original content to eventual action.

Hook → ResponseWhich hook produced a response?
Response → WebsiteDid the person open the tagged entry-door link?
Website → IntakeDid they start and complete the seven questions?
Intake → IntentDid they ask for help, country notification and/or nurture?
Intent → Human outcomeWas the lead contacted, qualified, referred, booked or closed?
Marketing optimization should eventually be based on downstream quality, not clicks alone. A hook that produces fewer but better-qualified Help Now leads may be more valuable than one that produces many low-intent visits.

11. Sharing and referral

Sharing is part of the acquisition engine, not a decorative website feature. Visitors should be able to share useful pages through WhatsApp, Telegram or copied links.

The desired loop is:

Curiosity → response → tagged entry door → education → intake → capture → useful content → share → new visitor → new capture.

Referral tracking should distinguish the immediate shared/referral visit from the original ancestor campaign wherever practical, without exposing the original visitor's personal information.

12. V2.1 backend — Google Sheets

Chosen lead store: Google Sheets. ThinkFlowFirst.com will send every completed intake to one controlled master sheet through a Google Apps Script webhook.

The V2.1 flow is:

ThinkFlowFirst.com → Render /api/lead → Google Apps Script → Google Sheet → Help Now alert / Country Waitlist / Keep Posted.

The Google Sheet will contain four operational tabs:

  • All Leads — the single master record for each visitor.
  • Help Now — dynamic view of people asking for human follow-up.
  • Country Waitlist — dynamic view of people asking to be notified when Flow First reaches their country.
  • Keep Posted — dynamic view of people who requested ongoing educational updates.

A person is stored only once in All Leads. The other tabs are views of that record rather than duplicates.

Help Now alert: when a visitor selects “I’d like help now,” the Google Apps Script sends an immediate email to nick81733@gmail.com. The email contains only name, country, entry door, contact details, received time and lead ID. Detailed seven-question health responses remain in the restricted Google Sheet.
Current implementation status: the Google Sheets receiver, tab structure and Help Now email logic have been prepared in the ThinkFlowFirst repository. The final live connection still requires the Google Sheet / Apps Script deployment to be authorized in the user's Google account and its deployment URL added to Render as LEAD_WEBHOOK_URL. Until that is completed, staging submissions continue to fall back to Render logs.

14. Daily operating checklist

Before publishingEvery content item has a clear hook ID and campaign ID.
When somebody respondsResponder identifies the topic and appropriate entry door.
Before sending a linkGenerate the tagged URL; do not strip tracking fields.
During follow-upKeep discussion helpful and human; do not make unsupported treatment claims.
Every dayReview new Help Now leads and unresolved follow-ups.
Every weekReview source → intake → intent quality by hook and channel.
Every campaign reviewScale, modify or stop based on downstream lead quality—not engagement vanity metrics alone.

15. Medical and privacy guardrails

  • Think Flow First is an educational/navigation platform, not an emergency service.
  • Do not promise cures, guaranteed recovery or treatment outcomes in responder messages.
  • Serious or sudden symptoms should be directed to appropriate urgent medical care.
  • Only collect information needed for the stated purpose.
  • Health-related responses should not be pasted casually into broad team chats or exposed in notification previews.
  • Access to detailed lead records should be limited to people who need it for follow-up.
  • Marketing consent and service/follow-up requests should remain distinguishable.

16. What success looks like

A successful Think Flow First funnel does not merely generate traffic. It creates a traceable chain of learning and permission:

We know what message attracted the person, why they responded, what they told us, what they want next, who is responsible for follow-up, and what eventually happened.

That gives Think Flow First a repeatable acquisition and learning system that can be carried from Cambodia to Malaysia and other markets while allowing the channels and entry doors to change by country.
