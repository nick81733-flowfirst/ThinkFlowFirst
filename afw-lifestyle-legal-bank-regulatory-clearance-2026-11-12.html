01. Executive decision brief

AFW wants to lease — not sell — approximately 50 demarcated countryside plots to expatriates and other eligible customers, while preserving underlying land ownership and the existing Sanctuary operation.

≈50Proposed lifestyle plots
600 m²Indicative plot size (20 × 30 m)
≈3 hectaresPlot area, excluding roads and common land
New Phase 1 option: test a genuinely separate 10–12 plot launch (6,000–7,200 m² of plot area), with the approximately 50-plot community remaining a possible future vision. The regulator must confirm what land counts toward the 10,000 m² threshold, whether connected phases are aggregated, and the required provincial or national approval. A smaller phase does not automatically mean no permit is required.
Three launch blockers to resolve: (1) correct real-estate regulatory classification and permits; (2) enforceable protection of long-term leaseholders despite the prior mortgage; (3) cadastral registration and legally permitted residential use for each proposed plot.

Mandate for counsel: identify the lowest-complexity lawful structure, with documentary confirmation from the competent authorities and a customer-protective bank arrangement. Do not assume that lease-only, no immediate title subdivision, or phased marketing creates an exemption.

This is a request for legal analysis, not a legal opinion. Final rights and conditions depend on the official Khmer texts, current enforcement practice, the actual title records and loan documents.

03. RPR — land-parcel developer licensing

Priority 1   Authority: Real Estate Business and Pawnshop Regulator (RPR), under Cambodia's Non-Bank Financial Services Authority; provincial Department of Economy and Finance where relevant.

Legal reference: Sub-Decree No. 50 (2023) and Prakas No. 047 (26 September 2023). Published legal reviews state that qualifying land-parcel projects below 10,000 m² require a provincial permit, while qualifying projects above 10,000 m² require an RPR licence. A smaller project is not automatically exempt. [R2, R3]

  1. Does an owner leasing already-titled, already-demarcated agricultural lifestyle plots, without sale or speculative home construction, constitute land-parcel development business or ordinary private leasing?
  2. Does the answer change because AFW maintains access roads, cuts grass, supplies community services or markets optional future housing?
  3. What counts toward the regulated project area — the approximately 30,000 m² of plots, common roads, other titles or the wider Sanctuary?
  4. If licensing applies, exactly which licence/permit, developer account, capital, guarantee, infrastructure and reporting requirements are triggered?
  5. Do the published land-parcel requirements of minimum capital equal to 40% of infrastructure cost and guarantee/deposit of 5% of land investment value apply here? If yes, on what valuation basis and phase?
  6. May AFW develop a separately surveyed Phase 1 of 10–12 plots under 10,000 m² and be assessed at provincial level despite a possible 50-plot master vision, or must connected phases be aggregated?
  7. May AFW publish concept marketing, conduct site tours, collect expressions of interest, take refundable reservations, take non-refundable deposits or execute leases, and at precisely which approval stage?
  8. Is a separate real-estate agency, property-management or other service licence needed where AFW leases and manages its own land?
  9. Please identify all approvals, filing forms, fees, expiry/renewal dates and likely timelines, with written confirmation.
Requested deliverable: RPR's written classification or formal approval pathway, including a precise statement of what AFW may do before receiving any licence.

03A. Phase 1 below 10,000 m² — proposed approach

Requires written determination   AFW is considering an initial 10–12 plot phase to test market demand and limit initial expenditure. This must be a genuine bounded development stage, not an artificial split designed to circumvent regulatory jurisdiction.

These figures exclude roads, drainage, shared land, setbacks and any broader parent-title areas that the authority may count. They are not a legal area certification.

Legal distinction: published commentary on Prakas No. 047 describes a provincial permit for qualifying land-lot projects below 10,000 m² and an RPR licence above 10,000 m². Below 10,000 m² is not a general exemption. The existence of phase-related provisions does not itself confirm that the planned larger development may be split to avoid national jurisdiction. [R2, R3]

Questions for RPR, Kampong Speu Provincial Department of Economy and Finance, and legal counsel

  1. Does AFW's lease-only, minimal-infrastructure project require a land-lot development permit at all, or qualify as ordinary private-land leasing?
  2. If it is regulated, may a genuinely bounded Phase 1 of 10–12 plots be considered separately for the threshold even though AFW may expand toward 50 plots?
  3. Would shared hard titles, access roads, utilities, an integrated masterplan, marketing, common facilities or future-phase intentions lead to aggregation?
  4. Is the area measured by the plots alone, the gross phase boundary including shared access, all land in the parent title, or the integrated scheme?
  5. Can RPR confirm provincial permit jurisdiction in writing? Which provincial department issues the permit and what is its legally correct application process?
  6. What capital, guarantee, infrastructure, survey, reporting and payment-handling obligations attach to a provincially permitted Phase 1?
  7. May AFW describe an unapproved future masterplan while leasing only the first approved phase? At which point may site visits, advertising and deposits begin?
  8. If AFW later extends the community, must it amend its permit, secure a new provincial permit, or obtain a national licence based on the total area?

Proposed safeguards

  • Obtain a surveyed Phase 1 boundary including roads and common facilities, not merely the total of individual plots.
  • Offer only approved first-stage plots; show all future phases as unapproved possibilities, without binding reservations.
  • Do not use administrative splitting or multiple contracts to evade licensing thresholds.
  • Separately satisfy bank consent, enforceable mortgage protection, cadastral lease registration, land use and house-building permissions.
Written clearance requested: classification as ordinary lease or regulated development; phase aggregation ruling; precise counted area; provincial versus national jurisdiction; applicable financial/infrastructure obligations; and the first lawful date for taking reservations or payments.

04. Mortgage bank — security and non-disturbance

Priority 1   Decision: whether AFW can offer each foreign lessee a credible, enforceable long-term right while the underlying land remains subject to an earlier registered mortgage (hypothec).

Cambodia's Civil Code recognizes registered hypothecs and long-term lease rights. Registration of a new lease is important but must not be assumed to override an existing, prior-ranking mortgage. [R1, R4]

  1. Which exact titles are security, for which loans, and are the proposed plot titles cross-collateralized with Sanctuary assets?
  2. Do any loan covenants prohibit or condition leasing, registration, subdivision, changes of use, construction or receipt/use of prepaid rent?
  3. Will the bank give explicit written consent for up to 50 individually registered long-term leases of up to 50 years?
  4. Will the bank release the original hard titles or coordinate with the cadastral office to register each lease?
  5. Can the bank execute an enforceable non-disturbance / recognition agreement protecting approved leases against lender enforcement? Would a purchaser following judicial sale also be bound?
  6. If the bank cannot assure protection, can specified existing titles be released from the mortgage against partial repayment or replacement collateral?
  7. Can there be a fixed release amount per title or plot, and what happens where plots sit within one title?
  8. Must the US$6,000 initial payments or prepaid community fees be received into a controlled account or applied to loan reduction?
  9. Would the bank consent to homes, fruit trees and other tenant-funded improvements? How would the bank's security extend to them?
  10. What alternative customer protection is acceptable pending full mortgage release — escrow, staged payment, conditional contracts or another locally enforceable arrangement?
Do not proceed on a simple bank consent letter alone. Require counsel to assess legal priority, remedies, registrability and enforceability through default and foreclosure. Get the bank's agreement in signed documents.

Requested deliverable: executed bank consent and enforceable protection, or an agreed mortgage-release arrangement; counsel's opinion on actual customer security.

05. Cadastral office — register the individual leases

Priority 1   Authority: Kampong Speu Provincial Department of Land Management, Urban Planning, Construction and Cadastre and the relevant district/cadastral office.

For registered long-term leases under the Civil Code, the maximum initial term is 50 years; a renewal may be made, with each renewed term capped at 50 years. Registration is central to enforceability against third parties. [R1, R4]

  1. Can a 600 m² portion of an existing hard title carry an individually registered perpetual/long-term lease without first subdividing ownership?
  2. Is the registered lease shown directly on the parent title, by separate lease certificate, or by another record?
  3. What survey, plan, coordinates, marker, frontage and road access requirements apply?
  4. Are existing mortgages an obstacle, and what originals, notarization, power of attorney and bank signatures must accompany registration?
  5. Can one title carry many individual long-term lease registrations, and what are any practical office limits?
  6. Who can sign for the landowner, and can eligible foreign individuals / foreign-controlled entities be registered as lessees?
  7. How are assignment, inheritance, replacement lessees and subleases recorded?
  8. What official survey, registration, stamp and administrative costs apply, and who pays them?
  9. If subdivision proves necessary, can titles be divided without any transfer of underlying ownership, and does the mortgage bank need to re-document security?
Requested deliverable: written requirements and fee schedule plus successful registration of one pilot 600 m² lease before offering 50 leases on the same model.

06. Land classification, housing and environment

Confirm locally   Authorities: Provincial land management / construction office; provincial environment office and other applicable regulators.

  1. What is the current cadastral / planning classification of each title, and does it permit farm plots, orchards, weekend cabins and full-time houses?
  2. Is change of land use, master-planning approval or other prior permission necessary for the intended residential community?
  3. Can the individual long-term lessee apply for a building permit, or must the freehold owner co-sign?
  4. What setbacks, road widths, drainage capacity, flood protection, emergency access and fire safety rules apply?
  5. Can each resident install standalone solar/battery, rainwater storage, well connections and decentralized wastewater treatment?
  6. Are there water extraction, effluent discharge, vegetation clearing or other environmental permits?
  7. Is environmental screening, an EIA or an approved management plan required given the project scale and surrounding land use?
  8. Does providing concept house designs or optional building contractors change AFW's classification as a developer?
  9. Can future Cambodian owners acquire individual hard titles after 10–15 years, and what subdivision/mortgage-release procedure would be required?

The 2019 Law on Construction and subsequent permit rules should be checked against official current texts and provincial administrative practice. [R5]

Requested deliverable: written land-use assessment, permitted home types, required permits and a workable self-build approval pathway.

07. Tax, collection and long-term service obligations

Tax opinion needed   Authority: General Department of Taxation (GDT), via Cambodian tax counsel/accountant.

  1. How is an upfront lease premium treated for a Cambodian corporate landowner? Is it rent, a premium, or another category for income tax, VAT, withholding or other taxes?
  2. What is the timing of taxable recognition of 1-, 2-, 5-, 15- or 30-year prepaid maintenance fees?
  3. Does the separate community-service contract genuinely qualify as payment for services, or would it be recharacterized as land rent based on substance?
  4. What treatment applies to the additional US$200 residence fee and US$100 per additional person, and to optional orchard services?
  5. Does Prakas No. 169 (2024) on rental tax apply given the identity and tax regime of the landholding entity? What different treatment applies under self-assessment? [R6, R7]
  6. Are there applicable stamp/registration levies, property taxes, unused-land taxes, or future transfer taxes borne by AFW or customers?
  7. How should overseas buyer payments, invoices, accounting, refunds, assignments and recognition of future service obligations be handled?
  8. Should a service-liability reserve or dedicated account be established for decades of prepaid upkeep?
Pricing decision: reconcile the US$38,800 campaign figure with the US$6,000 initial payment and the US$100/month base fee before any binding promotion. Avoid marketing a zero-price land transfer when only leasehold rights are granted.

Requested deliverable: payment-by-payment tax matrix, sample invoices, deposit/refund treatment and net cashflow model.

08. Contract package and consumer protections

Have one Cambodian property counsel own the coordinated contract suite. A standalone rental agreement is unlikely to cover the combined mortgage, community, construction and prepaid-services risks.

Specific counsel instructions

  1. Confirm if separate agreements are legally preferable or whether all obligations should be integrated; ensure there are no conflicting payment or default provisions.
  2. Explain the right to assign/sublet/inherit a registered long-term lease and the enforceable limits AFW can place on such rights.
  3. Specify what happens to houses, solar systems and agricultural improvements at expiry or earlier termination. Civil Code Article 254 is particularly important. [R1]
  4. Confirm enforceable remedies if a resident stops paying services fees, without assuming unilateral repossession is available.
  5. Define which Sanctuary facilities are included, paid, restricted or subject to availability; avoid promising perpetual free access.
  6. Clarify the status of future Cambodian title-purchase options: concept only unless and until subdivision, financing, price and tax arrangements are settled.

11. Suggested instructions to external counsel

Subject: AFW Sanctuary Lifestyle Community — pre-launch Cambodian real-estate, mortgage and tax diligence

We seek your advice on a proposed leasehold lifestyle community in Kampong Speu. We would like to test a genuine Phase 1 of approximately 10–12 plots potentially below 10,000 m², while retaining a possible longer-term vision of around 50 plots. Please determine whether Phase 1 may legally be assessed separately or must be aggregated with the wider proposal. The land is hard-titled and currently mortgaged; plots are demarcated but individual lease interests are not yet registered. We intend to retain freehold ownership, offer registered long-term leases (potentially up to 50 years), provide limited maintenance, and allow leaseholders to construct their own approved homes.

Please (1) determine whether the lease-only arrangement requires a land-lot development licence or other permits, (2) establish whether individual leases can be registered over portions of existing titles, (3) devise enforceable mortgage-bank protections, (4) confirm permitted residential use and self-build requirements, (5) advise on the tax and customer-protection treatment of upfront premiums and prepaid community fees, and (6) draft the required documentation.

We specifically request a written issues-and-risk memo distinguishing confirmed law, local practice, approvals outstanding and alternative structures, together with an actionable sequence and approximate government/professional costs. Please identify precisely what may be marketed and paid for before all approvals are complete.

Requested adviser outputs: legal memo; RPR written enquiry; bank term-sheet/consent proposal; cadastral pilot application; land-use memo; tax matrix; draft lease and community documents; go/no-go checklist.
